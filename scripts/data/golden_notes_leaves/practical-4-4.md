OVERVIEW
Self-learning stems. Write your answers first. Model answers follow each stem.

STEM 1
A 19-year-old primigravida at 34 weeks has BP 150/98, headache, and 1+ urine protein. She lives 18 km from the CHC. What is the immediate classification, where should she deliver, and which entitlements apply?

MODEL ANSWER 1
- Treat as pre-eclampsia until proven otherwise. This is high-risk. Do not send her home on a "next ANC" date.
- Delivery at a CEmONC facility (caesarean and blood available), not at a subcentre.
- JSSK: free referral transport, drugs, diet, diagnostics. SUMAN for respectful care. PMSMA if a specialist OPD is the next structured contact after stabilisation. Anti-hypertensives and magnesium as per obstetric protocol. Counsel danger signs.

STEM 2
A 7-month-old weighs 5.1 kg, MUAC 11.0 cm, no oedema, appetite good, no danger signs. Classify and place of care.

MODEL ANSWER 2
- SAM by MUAC (below 11.5 cm) without oedema and without IMNCI danger signs, appetite preserved: uncomplicated SAM. Community-based care with energy-dense food as per State SAM protocol, weekly follow-up, immunization, IYCF. NRC if appetite fails, medical complications, or oedema.

STEM 3
You see 12 fever cases in one hamlet in 3 days after heavy rain. First three public-health steps.

MODEL ANSWER 3
- Verify the outbreak (case definition, line list). Treat and investigate the first cases (malaria RDT/smear, dengue timing, leptospirosis if floods, water samples if watery diarrhoea).
- Control: breeding-site reduction or chlorination depending on the syndrome; IEC the same day.
- Notify IHIP/IDSP. Do not wait for a district meeting before the line list.

> **EXAM TIP:** Write the classification in the first line. Place of care second. Named entitlement or programme third.
