OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Four national programmes that often appear as short notes and that were missing as dedicated Library leaves: National Programme for Prevention and Control of Deafness (NPPCD), National Oral Health Programme (NOHP), National Programme for Palliative Care (NPPC), and National Programme for Prevention and Control of Fluorosis (NPPCF).

DEFINITIONS
ORAL HEALTH (WHO): A state of being free from chronic mouth and facial pain, oral and throat cancer, oral sores, birth defects such as cleft lip and palate, periodontal disease, tooth decay and tooth loss, and other diseases and disorders that affect the oral cavity.
PALLIATIVE CARE (WHO): An approach that improves the quality of life of patients and their families facing problems associated with life-threatening illness, through prevention and relief of suffering by early identification, impeccable assessment, and treatment of pain and other problems, physical, psychosocial, and spiritual.

[SN]National Programme for Prevention and Control of Deafness[/SN]

NPPCD
- Aim: prevent and control major causes of hearing impairment and deafness; the original 12th Plan wording was a 25 percent reduction in existing burden by 2012-17. Current answers should add that ear care is now among Comprehensive Primary Health Care services at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs.
- Components: manpower training from medical college ENT/audiology down to peripheral workers; capacity building of district hospital, Community Health Centre (CHC), and Primary Health Centre (PHC) ENT infrastructure; early detection and management; hearing aids as per scheme rules.
- Common exam causes of preventable deafness: chronic otitis media, ototoxicity, noise, congenital rubella, birth asphyxia, meningitis.

[SN]National Oral Health Programme[/SN]

NOHP
- Two most prevalent dental diseases in India: dental caries and periodontal disease. Periodontal disease links to diabetes; oral sepsis links to infective endocarditis teaching.
- Objectives: improve determinants of oral health; reduce morbidity from oral diseases; integrate oral health promotion with general care; encourage public-private partnership where appropriate.
- Service points: dental units at district hospitals and CHCs; oral health at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs; tobacco cessation linkage with the National Tobacco Control Programme; school oral health.

[SN]National Programme for Palliative Care[/SN]

[LAQ]National Programme for Palliative Care[/LAQ]

NPPC
- Palliative care is one of the services listed for Health and Wellness Centres / Ayushman Arogya Mandirs.
- Beneficiaries: cancer; HIV/AIDS; organ failure; chronic neurological disease; stroke and spinal injury; dementia; children with cerebral palsy or major birth defects.
- Goal: rational, quality pain relief and palliative care as an integral part of health care at all levels.
- Objectives: build capacity inside NP-NCD, National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly, National AIDS Control Programme, and NHM; legal and regulatory support so opioids are available for medical use without diversion; include palliative care in medical, nursing, and pharmacy curricula.
- Continuum: home, PHC/HWC, CHC, district hospital, tertiary centre. Caregivers are part of the unit of care.

[SN]National Programme for Prevention and Control of Fluorosis[/SN]

NPPCF
- Fluorosis follows excess fluoride in drinking water (and sometimes in food). Dental fluorosis in children; skeletal fluorosis in long-term exposure; non-skeletal complaints (dyspepsia, muscle).
- WHO guideline value commonly taught: 1.5 mg/L fluoride in drinking water. Indian drinking-water standard: 1.0 mg/L acceptable, 1.5 mg/L permissible in the absence of an alternative source (IS 10500 teaching).
- Strategies: alternative safe water (piped, rainwater, defluoridation); nutrition (calcium, vitamin C, antioxidants as taught in programme IEC); school screening for dental fluorosis; IEC in endemic districts.
- Link National Jal Jeevan Mission for household tap water in fluoride-affected habitations.
