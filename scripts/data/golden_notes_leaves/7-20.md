OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Two elimination programmes that sit outside the older National Health Programme list in many textbooks: the National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme (NVHCP) and the National Action Plan for Dog-Mediated Rabies Elimination by 2030 (NAPRE), implemented with the National Rabies Control Programme (NRCP).

DEFINITIONS
NATIONAL VIRAL HEPATITIS CONTROL PROGRAMME (NVHCP): A National Health Mission programme launched on 28 July 2018 (World Hepatitis Day) to prevent, detect, and treat viral hepatitis A, B, C, D, and E, aligned with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 3.3 (end viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030).
NATIONAL ACTION PLAN FOR DOG-MEDIATED RABIES ELIMINATION (NAPRE): The One Health guidance document for eliminating dog-mediated human rabies from India by 2030, jointly launched by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) and the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying.

[SN]National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme[/SN]

[LAQ]NVHCP: aims, structure, and 2030 target[/LAQ]

NVHCP
Launch: 28 July 2018, MoHFW, under NHM.

Aims
- Countrywide elimination of hepatitis C by 2030.
- Reduce the infected population and deaths from hepatitis B and C (cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma).
- Reduce risk, morbidity, and mortality from hepatitis A and E.

Components
1. Prevention: awareness, hepatitis B immunization (birth dose under Universal Immunization Programme, health-care workers, high-risk groups), safe blood, safe injections, water and sanitation for A and E.
2. Diagnosis and treatment: free diagnostics and drugs for hepatitis B and C.
3. Screening of pregnant women for HBsAg; institutional delivery; birth-dose hepatitis B; hepatitis B immunoglobulin (HBIG) within 24 hours for newborns of HBsAg-positive mothers.
4. Structure: National Viral Hepatitis Management Unit; State Viral Hepatitis Management Unit; District Viral Hepatitis Management Unit.
5. Hepatitis A and E outbreaks: Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme / Integrated Health Information Platform.

PIB (World Hepatitis Day 2026)
- More than 21 crore beneficiaries screened; more than 6 lakh patients treated; 1,153 treatment centres (target of one per district surpassed); 7.64 crore pregnant women screened; 2.24 lakh newborns of HBsAg-positive mothers given HBIG.
- Hepatitis B birth-dose coverage more than 92 percent. Pregnant-woman HBsAg screening still needs to reach 100 percent.

> **EXAM TIP:** Date 28 July 2018 → SDG 3.3 / HCV elimination 2030 → prevention plus free B and C treatment → pregnancy/HBIG/birth dose → 1,153 centres (2026 PIB).

[SN]NAPRE and National Rabies Control Programme[/SN]

[LAQ]Dog-mediated rabies elimination by 2030[/LAQ]

NAPRE / NRCP
- Rabies is almost always fatal after clinical onset and is preventable with timely post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP).
- Joint launch: World Rabies Day, 28 September 2021 (MoHFW with Animal Husbandry). States asked to notify rabies.
- Two components: human health (NCDC / NRCP: PEP, anti-rabies vaccine through the free drug initiative, surveillance of bites, IEC); animal health (Department of Animal Husbandry: mass dog vaccination, Animal Birth Control Rules 2023).
- Animal-side target taught from NAPRE: at least 70 percent anti-rabies vaccination coverage of dogs in a defined area, annually for three consecutive years, then maintain.
- States write a State Action Plan for Rabies Elimination (SAPRE). Joint steering committees at national, State, and district level.
- Animal Birth Control Rules, 2023: sterilisation plus anti-rabies vaccination of street dogs by the local authority.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Link NVHCP to injection safety and blood safety. Link NAPRE to One Health and to the rabies zoonoses topic.
