OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Climate, zoonoses, and respiratory-pandemic planning now have named national instruments: the National Programme on Climate Change and Human Health (NPCCHH), the National One Health Mission, the National One Health Programme for Prevention and Control of Zoonoses, and the National Pandemic Preparedness Plan for Respiratory Viruses (PPP-RV).

DEFINITIONS
CLIMATE CHANGE: Long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns, driven mainly by greenhouse-gas emissions, deforestation, and related human activity.
ONE HEALTH: An integrated, unifying approach that aims to sustainably balance and optimise the health of people, animals, and ecosystems.
PUBLIC HEALTH EMERGENCY OF INTERNATIONAL CONCERN (PHEIC): An extraordinary event under the International Health Regulations (2005) that constitutes a public health risk to other States through international spread and that may require a coordinated international response.

[LAQ]Climate change and NPCCHH[/LAQ]

CLIMATE CHANGE AND HEALTH
- Greenhouse gases: carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, ozone, chlorofluorocarbons. They absorb long-wave radiation from the earth (greenhouse effect).
- Direct health effects: heat stroke and heat-related death; injuries from floods, storms, drought; ultraviolet-related skin and eye disease.
- Indirect effects: water- and food-borne disease (cholera, leptospirosis after floods); vector-borne disease (malaria, dengue, Japanese encephalitis) as temperature and rainfall shift; undernutrition after crop failure; mental health and displacement.
- NPCCHH sits under the National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) / MoHFW, with State climate-health cells. Functions: vulnerability assessment, heat-health action plans, surveillance of climate-sensitive diseases, green and climate-resilient health facilities, intersectoral work with the Ministry of Environment.
- National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) is the air-pollution sibling (Ministry of Environment). Quote it when the question is PM2.5 and NCD, not when the question is heat waves.

[SN]National One Health Mission[/SN]

ONE HEALTH MISSION AND ZOONOSES PROGRAMME
- National One Health Mission coordinates human, animal, and wildlife health for zoonoses, antimicrobial resistance, and food safety.
- National One Health Programme for Prevention and Control of Zoonoses (NCDC): joint surveillance, outbreak investigation (Nipah, avian influenza, Kyasanur Forest Disease, leptospirosis, anthrax), laboratory networks, and district zoonosis committees.
- Rabies NAPRE is the flagship One Health disease plan (see 7-20).

[SN]National Pandemic Preparedness Plan for Respiratory Viruses[/SN]

PPP-RV
- National plan for any respiratory virus with pandemic potential. It uses lessons from 2009 influenza and coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19).
- Phases: pre-pandemic, pandemic, post-pandemic. The frame is meant to stay usable for a Public Health Emergency of International Concern caused by a respiratory virus.
- Pillars: One Health surveillance; health-system surge (beds, oxygen, diagnostics, vaccines, personal protective equipment); intersectoral command; risk communication; legal cover (Disaster Management Act, Epidemic Diseases Act as needed).
- World Health Organization Preparedness and Resilience for Emerging Threats (PRET) is the global sibling that also prioritises respiratory pathogens.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Heat-health action plans (Ahmedabad model is the classic exam city) now sit under NPCCHH. PPP-RV is the document to name if the question is "India's plan after COVID-19 for the next respiratory pandemic."
