OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Evidence-based public health uses the best available research, together with local epidemiology and values, to choose programmes. Systematic reviews and meta-analysis sit at the top of the evidence hierarchy for many questions. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve summarises how a test trades sensitivity against specificity.

DEFINITIONS
EVIDENCE-BASED PUBLIC HEALTH: The conscientious use of current best evidence in making decisions about community programmes, integrating research, local data, resources, and community values.
SYSTEMATIC REVIEW: A review that uses an explicit, reproducible method to find, appraise, and synthesise all studies that meet pre-stated eligibility criteria for a question.
META-ANALYSIS: A statistical method that combines numerical results from similar studies into a pooled estimate, often shown as a forest plot.
PRISMA: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses, the reporting checklist for a review.
ROC CURVE: A plot of sensitivity (true-positive rate) against 1 minus specificity (false-positive rate) at every possible cut-off of a continuous test.

STEPS OF A SYSTEMATIC REVIEW
1. Write the question (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, or PECO for exposures).
2. Protocol (register if required).
3. Search more than one database with documented strings.
4. Screen titles, abstracts, then full text with two reviewers.
5. Extract data and assess risk of bias.
6. Synthesise: narrative if studies differ too much; meta-analysis if they are similar enough.
7. Report with PRISMA flow (identified, screened, excluded, included).

META-ANALYSIS
- Forest plot: each study as a square, pooled diamond. Heterogeneity: I-squared and visual spread.
- Random-effects when studies differ; fixed-effect when they estimate one common effect and are homogeneous.
- Publication bias: funnel plot. Do not over-read a funnel with few studies.

ROC AND AREA UNDER THE CURVE
- Each point on the ROC is one cut-off. A test that hugs the top-left is better.
- Area under the curve (AUC): 0.5 is chance; nearer 1.0 is better discrimination.
- You cannot pick the "best" cut-off from AUC alone. Choose the cut-off that fits the programme (high sensitivity for a serious treatable disease; high specificity when a false positive is costly).
- Series and parallel combination of tests belong in the screening chapter (4).

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Programme choices (which vaccine, which screening test) should cite a systematic review or a WHO/MoHFW guideline that itself used one. Local SRS and NFHS numbers then size the problem. Critical appraisal of a paper is in 28-8.
