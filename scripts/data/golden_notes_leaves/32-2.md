OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Artificial intelligence (AI) in public health is the use of machine-learning and related tools for surveillance, imaging, triage, and programme targeting. IndiaAI Mission is the national AI programme. Health use-cases must still obey consent, Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and clinical accountability.

DEFINITIONS
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE: Computer systems that perform tasks that usually need human intelligence (pattern recognition, prediction, language).
INDIAAI MISSION: The Government of India mission to build compute, data, skills, and application ecosystems for AI, including public-good use-cases.

[SN]Artificial intelligence in public health[/SN]

USES
- Outbreak signal from IHIP, media, and laboratory streams.
- Chest X-ray and fundus screening support (TB, diabetic retinopathy) as decision support, not as a replacement for the clinician.
- Targeting of high-risk pregnant women or missed immunization from HMIS / U-WIN.
- Chatbots and Tele-MANAS-like first-contact triage (human escalation remains mandatory).

RISKS
- Bias if training data omit tribal, rural, or minority groups.
- Hallucinated advice if a language model is used without a protocol.
- Privacy: ABHA-linked data need fiduciary duties under DPDP.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Name IndiaAI Mission and ABDM together. Do not claim a national AI-diagnoses-TB programme unless a named, current MoHFW deployment is in the question.

> **EXAM TIP:** Define AI → three public-health uses → two risks (bias, privacy) → IndiaAI + DPDP. Keep the clinician in the loop.
