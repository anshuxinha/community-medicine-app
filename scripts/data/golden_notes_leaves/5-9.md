OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Three arthropod-borne infections that sit beside dengue, malaria, and filariasis in the exam but were not full notes in the Library: chikungunya, visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar), and Chandipura virus disease.

DEFINITIONS
CHIKUNGUNYA: An Aedes-transmitted viral fever caused by chikungunya virus (an alphavirus), classically presenting with high fever and severe, often prolonged, arthralgia.
VISCERAL LEISHMANIASIS (KALA-AZAR): A protozoal disease caused by Leishmania donovani, transmitted by the sandfly Phlebotomus argentipes, featuring prolonged fever, weight loss, splenomegaly, and darkening of the skin.
CHANDIPURA VIRUS DISEASE: An acute encephalitis syndrome caused by Chandipura vesiculovirus (a rhabdovirus), transmitted by sandflies, seen mainly in children.

[SN]Chikungunya[/SN]

CHIKUNGUNYA
- Agent: chikungunya virus (Togaviridae, alphavirus). Vector: Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus (day-biting).
- Incubation: about 2-12 days (commonly 3-7).
- Clinical: abrupt fever, severe polyarthralgia (hands, wrists, ankles), maculopapular rash, myalgia. Arthritis may persist for weeks to months. Deaths are uncommon; the burden is disability.
- Diagnosis: IgM ELISA, RT-PCR in the first few days.
- Treatment: supportive. No licensed vaccine in the Universal Immunization Programme. Aedes control is the public-health tool (source reduction, adulticiding during outbreaks).
- Differential: dengue (more haemorrhage and shock; chikungunya more arthritis). Co-circulation is common.

[SN]Kala-azar[/SN]

[LAQ]Epidemiology, prevention and control of kala-azar in India[/LAQ]

KALA-AZAR
- Agent: Leishmania donovani. Vector: Phlebotomus argentipes (female sandfly, rest in cattle sheds and cracks, bite at dusk/dawn).
- Reservoir in India: humans (anthroponotic). Post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL) is an important reservoir.
- Endemic belt: Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh (historically).
- Clinical: irregular fever, splenomegaly, anaemia, weight loss. PKDL: hypopigmented macules and nodules after treated or untreated visceral disease.
- Diagnosis: rK39 rapid test, smear/NAAT from bone marrow or spleen in specialised centres.
- Treatment: follow current National Vector Borne Disease Control / NCVBDC protocol (liposomal amphotericin B is the usual first-line teaching; miltefosine in selected regimens). Do not quote a dose from memory if the circular has moved.
- Elimination target: less than 1 case per 10,000 population at block level. Indoor residual spray, insecticide-treated nets, PKDL search, and complete treatment are the four operational pillars.

[SN]Chandipura virus disease[/SN]

CHANDIPURA
- Named after Chandipura village (Maharashtra), 1965 isolate. Rhabdoviridae.
- Vector: sandflies (Phlebotomus). Children are the usual cases.
- Clinical: acute encephalitis syndrome: high fever, altered sensorium, seizures, rapid progression. Case fatality in outbreaks is high.
- 2024: large AES clusters in Gujarat and neighbouring States were investigated as Chandipura by national agencies. Use "outbreak year 2024" in the answer; do not invent a national case total.
- Control: sandfly control, AES surveillance, supportive paediatric intensive care, no specific antiviral in programme use.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- All three sit under NCVBDC for outbreak support. Kala-azar has an elimination programme; chikungunya and Chandipura are outbreak responses plus entomology.
