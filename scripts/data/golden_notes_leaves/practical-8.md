OVERVIEW
High-yield pairs. One line each. Grouped so a viva can stay inside one domain.

EPIDEMIOLOGY
- Isolation: ill or infected person. Quarantine: well but exposed person.
- Incidence: new cases, rate, cohort. Prevalence: all current cases, proportion, cross-section.
- Endemic: constant presence. Outbreak/epidemic: excess over expected. Pandemic: several countries or continents.
- Source: from which the agent passes to the host. Reservoir: living or non-living where the agent lives and multiplies.
- Control: reduction to a locally acceptable level. Elimination: zero incidence in a defined area with continued measures. Eradication: permanent zero worldwide. Extinction: agent no longer in nature or laboratory.
- Association: statistical togetherness. Causation: Hill's aspects after bias and confounding are addressed.
- Confounder: associated with exposure and outcome, not on the causal path. Effect modifier: the effect size changes across strata.
- Screening test: among apparently well. Diagnostic test: among those with a clinical indication.

VACCINES
- Vaccination: the act of giving the antigen. Immunization: the immune response (or the programme).
- Live versus killed: live can mimic infection and usually fewer doses; killed cannot cause the disease but may need boosters.
- Vaccine efficacy: trial. Effectiveness: field. Efficiency: cost per result.
- Left-outs: never started the schedule. Drop-outs: started, missed later doses.
- Cold chain: +2 to +8°C (most UIP antigens). Reverse cold chain: samples from field to lab. Warm chain: controlled-temperature chain for selected heat-stable vaccines where policy allows.

RCH
- BEmONC: basic emergency obstetric and newborn care. CEmONC: comprehensive, including caesarean and blood.
- Newborn care corner (labour room) versus NBSU (CHC) versus SNCU (district).
- Preterm: before 37 completed weeks. Small for date: weight below the gestational-age standard (may be term).

NUTRITION AND ENVIRONMENT
- Fortification: adding nutrients to a staple used by the whole population. Supplementation: giving a product to a target group.
- Sanitation: safe disposal of excreta and waste. Hygiene: personal practices (handwashing).
- Contamination: presence of an agent where it should not be. Pollution: adverse environmental change. Infestation: arthropods or worms on/in the body or fomites.

STATS
- Standard deviation: spread of observations. Standard error: precision of a mean (or proportion).
- Confidence interval: range for a population parameter. Prediction interval: range for a new observation.
- Direct standardisation: apply study rates to a standard population. Indirect: apply standard rates to the study population (SMR).

PROGRAMMES AND MANAGEMENT
- Monitoring: tracking activities against a plan. Evaluation: judging whether objectives were met, often with an external look.
- IEC: information, education, communication. BCC: behaviour change communication. SBCC: social and behaviour change communication (norms as well as individual behaviour).
- Pull: indent from below. Push: supply sent from above without a full indent.

> **EXAM TIP:** Two columns, one sentence each. Isolation/quarantine, incidence/prevalence, left-out/drop-out, BEmONC/CEmONC, SD/SE are the first five to memorise.
