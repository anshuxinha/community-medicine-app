OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Geographic Information Systems (GIS) store, analyse, and display data that have a location. Remote sensing collects information about the earth's surface without physical contact, usually from satellites or aircraft. Both are tools for surveillance, targeting, and disaster work.

DEFINITIONS
GEOGRAPHIC INFORMATION SYSTEM (GIS): A computer system that captures, stores, analyses, and displays geographically referenced data as layers on a map.
REMOTE SENSING: Acquisition of information about an object or area from a distance, typically satellite or aerial sensors measuring reflected or emitted radiation.

[SN]GIS and remote sensing in public health[/SN]

USES
- Outbreak mapping: spot clustering of cases (cholera along a river, dengue by ward).
- Vector habitat: satellite vegetation, water, and temperature layers to predict malaria or kala-azar risk.
- Health-facility planning: distance to the nearest Ayushman Arogya Mandir or blood bank; identifying unserved habitations.
- Census 2027 Houselisting Block Creator uses satellite imagery to draw blocks.
- Disaster: flood extent, cyclone track, damaged roads, where to drop supplies.
- Environmental health: air-quality grids, fluoride or arsenic-affected habitations.

LIMITATIONS
- A map is only as good as the case definition and the geo-coding. Missing addresses create false blanks.
- Satellite indices (NDVI, land-surface temperature) are proxies, not diagnoses.
- Privacy: household-level health points need aggregation before public maps (DPDP Act, 2023).

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Integrated Health Information Platform (IHIP) supports map views of outbreaks. National Vector Borne Disease Control uses GIS for API and foci. Bhuvan and Indian Space Research Organisation products are the usual teaching names for Indian satellite layers.

> **EXAM TIP:** Define GIS and remote sensing → four uses (outbreak, vector, facility, disaster) → one limitation → one India platform (IHIP or Census HLBC).
