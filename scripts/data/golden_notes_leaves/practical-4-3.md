OVERVIEW
Two worked clinico-social cases. Long case: whole family plus environment. Short case: the index patient plus the immediate social diagnosis.

CASE A: ANTENATAL WOMAN (LONG)
- Index: 24-year-old G2P1L1 at 28 weeks, booked, Hb 11.2 g/dL, BP normal. Nuclear BPL family. Husband seasonal migrant.
- Social diagnosis: second pregnancy in a BPL nuclear household; migrant husband (delayed care if she depends on him for transport); one under-five sibling (competing time); adequate latrine and LPG.
- Problems: possible future anaemia if IFA adherence slips; no one at home for labour transport at night.
- Plan: JSSK vehicle number on the wall; ASHA birth-preparedness; Td/IFA/calcium; GDM screen; PPIUCD counselling; sibling immunization check; migrant-husband TB/NCD screen when he returns.

CASE B: ADULT WITH SPUTUM-POSITIVE TB (SHORT)
- Index: 32-year-old man, 4 weeks cough, NAAT rifampicin-sensitive, BMI 17.8, smokes bidi, lives in one-room house with wife and two children.
- Social diagnosis: infectious TB in a crowded one-room home; undernutrition; tobacco; wage loss; two child contacts.
- Plan: daily FDC as notified, Ni-kshay Poshan, smoke-free house, window open, children screened (symptom, CXR/NAAT as per paediatric NTEP), HIV and sugar, alcohol brief advice, 108 if haemoptysis.

HOW TO END EITHER CASE
- Clinico-social diagnosis in one sentence.
- Three problems (medical, social, environmental).
- Three actions you will take this week (not a wishlist of 20 schemes).

> **EXAM TIP:** Long case: family table then environment then index. Short case: index then household. Always name contacts in TB and birth-preparedness in ANC.
