OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Surface infections that complete the communicable-disease gist: trachoma, yaws, scabies, and pediculosis. Tetanus and leprosy remain in 5-7. HIV and STDs remain in 5-8. Yaws as a national eradication programme is also in 7-19; this section is the disease note.

DEFINITIONS
TRACHOMA: A chronic keratoconjunctivitis caused by Chlamydia trachomatis (serovars A, B, Ba, C), spread by direct contact, fomites, and eye-seeking flies; repeated infection scars the eyelid and may cause blindness.
YAWS: A non-venereal treponematosis caused by Treponema pallidum subspecies pertenue, spread by direct skin contact, seen in humid rural foci.
SCABIES: Infestation of the skin by the mite Sarcoptes scabiei var. hominis, spread by prolonged skin contact.
PEDICULOSIS: Infestation with lice (Pediculus humanus capitis, P. humanus corporis, or Pthirus pubis).

[SN]Trachoma[/SN]

TRACHOMA
- Agent: C. trachomatis A-C. Reservoir: children with active disease. Vector-like role: Musca sorbens (eye fly) plus dirty faces and crowded housing.
- SAFE strategy (WHO): Surgery for trichiasis, Antibiotics (azithromycin mass treatment where indicated), Facial cleanliness, Environmental improvement (water, latrines).
- India: National Programme for Control of Blindness and Visual Impairment includes trachoma. Several previously endemic districts have been declared free of public-health-level trachoma after surveys; maintain SAFE and surveillance rather than claiming global eradication.

[SN]Yaws[/SN]

YAWS
- Early: papilloma (mother yaw), then multiple skin lesions. Late: periostitis, sabre tibia, gangosa (destructive rhinopharyngitis) if untreated.
- Not sexually transmitted. Children in humid, forested, poor-hygiene settings.
- India: National Yaws Eradication Programme. Last cases in the early 2000s; certified free. Rumour registration and school surveys continue in old foci (see 7-19). Single-dose azithromycin is the current mass-treatment tool in remaining global foci.

[SN]Scabies[/SN]

SCABIES
- Burrows in finger webs, wrists, genitalia; nocturnal itch. Crusted (Norwegian) scabies in immunocompromised hosts sheds huge mite loads.
- Treatment: topical permethrin 5% (usual first-line teaching) or oral ivermectin in selected outbreak and crusted cases. Treat all close contacts. Wash clothes and bedding.
- Public health: overcrowding, boarding schools, prisons, camps. Mass drug administration with ivermectin appears in some NTD packages; follow the current national circular.

[SN]Pediculosis[/SN]

PEDICULOSIS
- Head louse: children, shared combs. Body louse: clothing seams; can transmit epidemic typhus, relapsing fever, and trench fever in historical and camp settings. Pubic louse: sexual contact.
- Control: permethrin or other pediculicide; combing; wash or bag clothing; body-louse control is clothing hygiene, not only a shampoo.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Trachoma is a blindness-programme question. Yaws is an elimination-maintenance question. Scabies and lice are school-health and camp questions. Do not mix yaws (non-venereal treponeme) with syphilis.

> **EXAM TIP:** Trachoma: SAFE. Yaws: non-venereal, India certified free, azithromycin. Scabies: permethrin plus contacts. Body louse: name the three historic diseases.
