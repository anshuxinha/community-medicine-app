OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Disease-wise public health indicators sit in programme reports, not in a single SRS table. Quote the programme year. This section lists high-yield national figures for tuberculosis, malaria, HIV, immunization, and non-communicable disease screening without inventing unpublished rates.

DEFINITIONS
INCIDENCE (TB): New and relapse tuberculosis cases estimated per 100,000 population per year (World Health Organization Global TB Report method, used in India TB reports).
ANNUAL PARASITE INCIDENCE (API): Confirmed malaria cases per 1,000 population per year.

[SN]Disease-wise public health indicators in India[/SN]

TUBERCULOSIS
- Use the latest India TB Report / WHO Global TB Report pair. India remains the country with the largest share of global incident TB.
- Programme indicators that belong in an answer: notification (total and microbiological), treatment success, missing cases closed by Ni-kshay, and drug-resistant TB enrolment.
- Do not quote an incidence number from memory if the year is not in front of you. Name the report.

MALARIA
- API and deaths are published by the National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control (NCVBDC). India is in the National Framework for Malaria Elimination 2016-2030, with the National Strategic Plan 2023-2027 for States.
- Zero indigenous cases is the elimination endpoint. Several States already report very low API.

HIV
- National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO) Sankalak reports the 95-95-95 cascade. NACP-V (2021-26) progress cited for 2024-25: 83.9-86.6-97.6 versus 70.7-82.5-84.9 in 2020-21.
- Sentinel surveillance and NFHS HIV testing modules are separate from programme cascade numbers.

IMMUNIZATION
- NFHS-6 full immunization (card): 87.1 percent among children 12-23 months (83.8 percent in NFHS-5).
- Public facility share of most vaccinations: 95.6 percent.
- Universal Immunization Programme coverage is also tracked in HMIS and U-WIN. Prefer NFHS for survey coverage and HMIS for session doses.

NON-COMMUNICABLE DISEASES
- NP-NCD population-based screening from 30 years: hypertension, diabetes, oral, breast, and cervical cancers.
- NFHS-6: overweight or obese 30.7 percent of women and 27.3 percent of men aged 15-49.
- WHO STEPS State surveys supply risk-factor prevalence where a State paper is asked.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- For a mixed "current status of diseases of public health importance" short note: one TB line, one malaria API line, one HIV 95-95-95 line, one full-immunization line, one NCD screening line. Source each.

> **EXAM TIP:** Five diseases in a table (indicator, latest figure, source). Skip any cell you cannot source. Examiners mark a blank cell above a guessed rate.
