OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Climate change is a long-term shift in temperature and weather patterns. Health effects are direct (heat, storms, ultraviolet radiation) and indirect (water, food, vectors, migration). The National Programme on Climate Change and Human Health (NPCCHH) is the named Indian programme. Programme detail is in 7-22. This section is the environmental science and the heat-health package.

DEFINITIONS
GREENHOUSE EFFECT: Incoming solar radiation is transmitted by the atmosphere, while long-wave radiation from the earth's surface is absorbed by greenhouse gases, warming the troposphere.
HEAT STROKE: A medical emergency with core temperature typically above 40°C and central nervous system dysfunction, after exposure to environmental heat.

GREENHOUSE GASES
- Carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), nitrous oxide (N2O), ozone (O3), chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).
- Drivers: fossil-fuel combustion, deforestation, agriculture, industry.

HEALTH IMPACT MAP
- Heat and cold: heat exhaustion, heat stroke, cardiovascular events.
- Extreme weather: floods, cyclones, drought; injuries, drowning, mental health, displacement.
- Water and food: cholera, cryptosporidiosis, leptospirosis after floods; undernutrition after crop failure.
- Air: ozone and particulate matter; asthma and ischaemic heart disease.
- Vectors: malaria, dengue, Japanese encephalitis as temperature and rainfall envelopes shift.
- Ultraviolet radiation: cataract, skin cancer.

HEAT-HEALTH (EXAM PACKAGE)
- Heat-health action plan: early warning, public messaging, cooling spaces, changed work hours, oral rehydration, hospital surge for heat stroke.
- Ahmedabad is the classic Indian city plan taught in examinations.
- Differentiate heat cramp, heat exhaustion, heat syncope, and heat stroke (see occupational health for the industrial list).

NPCCHH POINTER
- State climate-health cells, climate-sensitive disease surveillance, climate-resilient health facilities (water, power backup, waste), and heat action support. Full programme note: 7-22.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Poor households, outdoor workers, elderly, and people with heart or kidney disease carry the heat burden. National Clean Air Programme addresses PM2.5; do not merge NCAP and NPCCHH in one undifferentiated paragraph.
