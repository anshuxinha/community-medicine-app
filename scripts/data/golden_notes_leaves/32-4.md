OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Adult immunization is not a second Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) schedule. UIP is child- and pregnant-woman-centred. Adults need risk-based vaccines. Indian Consensus on Adult Immunization and National Technical Advisory Group on Immunization (NTAGI) product decisions are the teaching sources. Quote the latest NTAGI minutes if a specific product year is asked.

DEFINITIONS
ADULT IMMUNIZATION: Vaccination of persons 18 years and older, including pregnant women for selected antigens already in UIP, older adults, and adults with occupational, travel, or medical-risk indications.
NTAGI: The National Technical Advisory Group on Immunization, India's highest advisory body on immunization policy.

UIP TOUCHPOINTS IN ADULTS
- Td (tetanus-diphtheria) in pregnancy (UIP).
- Hepatitis B birth dose is for the newborn, not the adult; adults at risk get hepatitis B as a recommended vaccine outside the infant schedule.
- COVID-19 vaccination of adults followed NTAGI and National Expert Group on Vaccine Administration for COVID-19 (NEGVAC) circulars during the pandemic years.

COMMONLY RECOMMENDED ADULT ANTIGENS (CONSENSUS TEACHING)
- Influenza: annual, especially elderly, pregnant women, health workers, chronic lung or heart disease.
- Pneumococcal: older adults and asplenia / chronic disease as per product label and consensus.
- Hepatitis B: health workers, dialysis, high-risk sexual or injecting exposure, if not already immune.
- Td or Tdap boosters: every 10 years in many consensus tables; pregnancy Td as per UIP.
- Human papillomavirus (HPV): catch-up in young women and, where policy expands, boys; not yet a universal adult UIP antigen.
- Varicella, herpes zoster, hepatitis A: selected adults.
- Travel: yellow fever (International Health Regulations certificate), typhoid, meningococcal, as destination requires.
- Occupational: hepatitis B and influenza for health workers; rabies pre-exposure for selected veterinary and wildlife staff.

PRINCIPLES
- Check prior doses and serology where it changes the decision (hepatitis B).
- Live vaccines: caution in pregnancy and severe immunosuppression.
- Adult coverage is low. Health-worker hepatitis B is the classic audit indicator.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Do not draw a fake "National Adult Immunization Schedule" with the same boxes as the child UIP table. Write "risk-based, consensus plus NTAGI." Pregnant-woman Td remains UIP.

> **EXAM TIP:** "No separate UIP adult chart" → pregnancy Td → health-worker hepatitis B and influenza → elderly influenza/pneumococcal → travel yellow fever. Table by risk group, not by month.
