OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Additional zoonoses that the communicable-disease gist treats as full short notes: plague, leptospirosis, Kyasanur Forest Disease (KFD), and Nipah virus disease. Rabies, yellow fever, Japanese encephalitis, and rickettsial infections remain in 5-6.

DEFINITIONS
PLAGUE: A zoonosis caused by Yersinia pestis, maintained in rodents and their fleas, presenting as bubonic, septicaemic, or pneumonic disease.
LEPTOSPIROSIS: A spirochaetal zoonosis caused by pathogenic Leptospira, acquired from water or soil contaminated with infected animal urine.
KYASANUR FOREST DISEASE: A tick-borne flavivirus disease of the Western Ghats, with a sylvatic cycle in monkeys and Haemaphysalis ticks.
NIPAH VIRUS DISEASE: A henipavirus zoonosis with fruit-bat reservoir, capable of person-to-person spread, presenting as encephalitis and sometimes pneumonia.

[SN]Plague[/SN]

PLAGUE
- Agent: Yersinia pestis. Vector: Xenopsylla cheopis (rat flea). Reservoir: wild and commensal rodents.
- Bubonic: painful bubo, fever. Pneumonic: person-to-person by droplets; the form that starts a human epidemic.
- India: historical Deccan and Himalayan foci; last major outbreak teaching case is Surat 1994. Surveillance continues in known foci.
- Control: rodent and flea control, streptomycin/gentamicin as classic therapy, isolation of pneumonic cases, chemoprophylaxis of contacts.

[SN]Leptospirosis[/SN]

LEPTOSPIROSIS
- Source: rats, cattle, dogs, pigs. Entry: abraded skin, mucosa, conjunctiva. Floods and paddy work are classic.
- Clinical: fever, calf myalgia, conjunctival suffusion, jaundice, renal failure (Weil disease).
- Diagnosis: IgM ELISA, MAT in reference labs, PCR early.
- Treatment: penicillin or doxycycline as taught; start without waiting for MAT.
- Prevention: boots in flooded fields, rodent control, doxycycline prophylaxis in short high-risk exposures as per outbreak protocol.

[SN]Kyasanur Forest Disease[/SN]

KFD
- Western Ghats of Karnataka and adjoining States. Monkeys die in the forest (sentinel). Haemaphysalis spinigera.
- Biphasic fever, haemorrhage, encephalitis in a fraction of cases.
- Formalin-inactivated KFD vaccine is used in endemic taluks for residents and forest workers as per State schedule (two primary doses, boosters). Tick avoidance: clothing, repellents, no monkey handling.

[SN]Nipah virus disease[/SN]

[LAQ]Nipah virus: reservoir, outbreaks, and control[/LAQ]

NIPAH
- Reservoir: Pteropus fruit bats. Intermediate hosts in some outbreaks: pigs (Malaysia 1998). India: Kerala outbreaks (2018, 2019, 2021, 2023 and later clusters as notified). Named from Sungai Nipah, Malaysia.
- Spread: bat-contaminated fruit or date palm sap; person-to-person by droplets and close contact (hospital amplification is the exam danger).
- Clinical: fever, altered sensorium, pneumonia, high case fatality.
- Control: no licensed vaccine in UIP. Early isolation, PPE, contact tracing, ban on sap where implicated, fruit washing, bat-human interface reduction. One Health (human, animal, wildlife).

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- Plague and KFD are geographically focal. Leptospirosis is seasonal with the monsoon. Nipah is rare, high-fatality, and a hospital-infection-control question.
