OVERVIEW
Viva lists that examiners use as rapid-fire. Keep one official source in mind for dates and numbers. Helplines below are national; State numbers vary.

SELECTED PUBLIC HEALTH DAYS
- 30 January: Anti-Leprosy Day in India (Martyrs' Day; Mahatma Gandhi).
- 4 February: World Cancer Day.
- 24 March: World Tuberculosis Day.
- 7 April: World Health Day (WHO founding 1948).
- 25 April: World Malaria Day.
- 31 May: World No Tobacco Day.
- 5 June: World Environment Day.
- 14 June: World Blood Donor Day.
- 28 July: World Hepatitis Day.
- 1-7 August: World Breastfeeding Week.
- 1-7 September (India): National Nutrition Week.
- 28 September: World Rabies Day.
- 10 October: World Mental Health Day (Tele-MANAS launch anniversary).
- 14 November: World Diabetes Day.
- 1 December: World AIDS Day.
- 10 December: Human Rights Day.

HELPLINES (NATIONAL)
- 112: emergency.
- 108: ambulance (many States).
- 104: health information / doctor advice (State health).
- 1075: COVID-era national health helpline; confirm current use if asked.
- 14416: Tele-MANAS mental health.
- 14446: Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan.
- 1098: Childline.
- 181: women helpline (many States).
- 1078: disaster (NDMA teaching number in some lists).

VECTORS (ONE LINE)
- Anopheles: malaria.
- Culex quinquefasciatus: bancroftian filariasis.
- Culex tritaeniorhynchus: Japanese encephalitis.
- Aedes aegypti: dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever.
- Phlebotomus argentipes: kala-azar.
- Xenopsylla cheopis: plague.
- Glossina: African trypanosomiasis (not Indian).
- Cyclops: guinea worm (historical; India certified free).
- Haemaphysalis spinigera: Kyasanur Forest Disease.
- Sandfly (Phlebotomus): Chandipura virus.

INCUBATION PERIODS (CLASSIC TEACHING RANGES)
- Cholera: hours to 5 days (usually 1-2).
- Diphtheria: 2-6 days.
- Pertussis: 7-14 days.
- Measles: 10-14 days to rash (about 8-12 to fever).
- Chickenpox: 14-16 days.
- Mumps: 14-18 days.
- Rubella: 14-21 days.
- Hepatitis A: 15-45 days (about 30).
- Hepatitis B: 30-180 days (about 90).
- Polio: 7-14 days.
- Tetanus: 3-21 days (average 10).
- Rabies: 3-8 weeks typical; days to years possible.
- Malaria falciparum: about 9-14 days.
- Dengue: 4-7 days (3-14).

ELIMINATED OR ERADICATED FROM INDIA (MAINTENANCE STILL NEEDED)
- Smallpox: global eradication 1980.
- Guinea worm: certified free.
- Yaws: national eradication, certified free.
- Polio: WHO South-East Asia certified 2014; keep AFP surveillance.
- Maternal and neonatal tetanus: eliminated as a public-health problem (2015).
- Trachoma as a public-health problem: several Indian declarations after surveys; maintain SAFE.

> **EXAM TIP:** Days: 7 April WHO, 24 March TB, 1 December AIDS, 10 October mental health. Helplines: 14416 versus 14446. Vectors: Aedes four viruses. Incubation: measles 10-14, Hep B months, rabies weeks.
