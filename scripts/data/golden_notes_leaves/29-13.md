OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Three newer instruments that post-date the classic public-health law list: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023; the Animal Birth Control Rules, 2023; and the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022.

DEFINITIONS
DIGITAL PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION ACT, 2023 (DPDP ACT): The principal Indian statute on processing digital personal data, built on consent, purpose limitation, and duties of data fiduciaries.
ANIMAL BIRTH CONTROL RULES, 2023: Rules under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act that require local authorities to sterilise and anti-rabies-vaccinate street dogs, replacing the 2001 dog rules.
E-WASTE (MANAGEMENT) RULES, 2022: Rules under the Environment (Protection) Act that apply extended producer responsibility to electrical and electronic equipment waste.

[SN]Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023[/SN]

DPDP ACT, 2023
- Applies to digital personal data processed in India, and to processing outside India if it relates to offering goods or services in India.
- Data principal: the individual. Data fiduciary: the entity that determines purpose and means.
- Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous. Notice in clear language.
- Certain legitimate uses (including specified State uses) are listed in the Act. Health-care providers handling digital records must still meet consent and security duties unless a listed use applies.
- Data Protection Board of India is the enforcement body. Penalties are administrative.
- Exam link: Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, hospital information systems, research databases.

[SN]Animal Birth Control Rules 2023[/SN]

ABC RULES, 2023
- Local authority is responsible for street-dog population management.
- Capture-sterilise-vaccinate-release. Anti-rabies vaccination is integral, not optional.
- Feeding and cruelty provisions sit with animal-welfare law; public-health exam angle is rabies (NAPRE) plus nuisance and bite prevention.
- Replaces ABC (Dogs) Rules, 2001, in teaching tables.

[SN]E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022[/SN]

E-WASTE RULES, 2022
- Electrical and electronic equipment at end of life: producer extended responsibility, collection targets, authorised dismantlers and recyclers.
- Bulk consumers (including large hospitals) have return duties.
- Distinct from Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016: a discarded monitor is e-waste; a blood-soaked gauze is biomedical waste. Do not colour-code e-waste into yellow/red BMW bags.

INDIAN CONTEXT:
- DPDP is the privacy answer for ABDM. ABC Rules 2023 is the statute line in a rabies/One Health answer. E-waste 2022 is the hospital-engineering answer, not the BMW colour chart.

> **EXAM TIP:** One Act/Rules, year, one duty-holder, one public-health link. DPDP → consent and ABHA. ABC → local body and rabies. E-waste → EPR, not BMW.
