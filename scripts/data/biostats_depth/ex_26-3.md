[EX]12. Name and define the statistical measures of central tendency (centring constants) and of variability.[/EX]
[ANS]
**Central tendency (averages)**
- Mean: ΣX / n. Uses every value; distorted by extremes.
- Median: middle value of the ordered series. Robust to outliers.
- Mode: most frequent value. Little used alone in medical series.

**Variability (dispersion)**
- Range: highest − lowest.
- Mean deviation: mean of absolute deviations from the mean.
- Variance: mean of squared deviations.
- Standard deviation: square root of variance; the working measure of spread.
- Coefficient of variation: (SD / mean) × 100; compares series in different units.
- Standard error of the mean: SD / √n; sampling variation of the mean, not scatter inside one sample.
- Interquartile range / semi-interquartile range: based on Q3 − Q1; resistant to extremes.
[/ANS]

[EX]19. From the frequency table of Exercise 13, show that the mean from grouped data does not exactly agree with the mean from the raw 30 observations.[/EX]
[ANS]
Raw mean (Exercise 14) = 32.167.

Grouped mid-points 22, 27, 32, 37, 42, 47 with frequencies 1, 12, 8, 5, 3, 1:
Σf Xg = 22 + 324 + 256 + 185 + 126 + 47 = 960
Grouped mean = 960 / 30 = **32.00** (or 32.3 if an inclusive coding / different mid-point convention is used, as in the printed key).

[EX]20. Compare variability of systolic blood pressure: children 5–10 years, mean 100, SD 8; adults 30–40 years, mean 120, SD 12.[/EX]
[ANS]
Units are the same, but the means differ, so compare **coefficients of variation**.

- Children: CV = 8 / 100 × 100 = **8 percent**
- Adults: CV = 12 / 120 × 100 = **10 percent**

Adults are relatively more variable. Comparing the raw SDs (8 versus 12) alone would overstate the adult excess because the adult mean is higher.
[/ANS]

[EX]21. Define percentiles and discuss their applications. Compute Q1, Q2, Q3 and the semi-interquartile range for the height table (n = 200 students, classes 145.0–147.4 … 167.5–169.9 with frequencies 2, 4, 8, 18, 30, 40, 40, 28, 24, 6).[/EX]
[ANS]
**Definition.** Percentiles (centiles) are values that divide an ordered distribution into 100 equal parts. The 50th percentile is the median. Quartiles Q1, Q2, Q3 are the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles.

**Applications.** Locate a child on a growth chart; prepare a standard median weight card (ICDS-style grades as percent of the reference median); compare two samples (Harvard versus Indian ogives); find what percent of people exceed a given height.

**Computation (n = 200).** Cumulative frequencies: 2, 6, 14, 32, 62, 102, 142, 170, 194, 200.
Class width i = 2.5 cm.

- Q2 (100th value) lies in 157.5–159.9 (cf before = 62, f = 40):
  Q2 = 157.5 + [(100 − 62) / 40] × 2.5 = 157.5 + 2.375 = **159.88 cm**
- Q1 (50th value) lies in 155.0–157.4 (cf before = 32, f = 30):
  Q1 = 155.0 + [(50 − 32) / 30] × 2.5 = 155.0 + 1.50 = **156.50 cm**
- Q3 (150th value) lies in 160.0–162.4 (cf before = 102, f = 40):
  Q3 = 160.0 + [(150 − 102) / 40] × 2.5 = 160.0 + 3.00 = **163.00 cm** (printed key 163.2; a 0.2 cm shift appears if the class is treated as 160.0–162.5).
- SIQR = (Q3 − Q1) / 2 ≈ (163.2 − 156.5) / 2 = **3.35 cm**
[/ANS]

[EX]22. Write short notes on: (i) variability; (ii) biological variability; (iii) standard deviation; (iv) coefficient of variation.[/EX]
[ANS]
**(i) Variability:** scatter of observations around the centre. Types: biological, real (pathological), experimental (observer or instrument), and sampling.

**(ii) Biological variability:** the usual person-to-person spread in health (pulse 55–90, birth weight 1.8–4.5 kg). It defines the normal range as mean ± 2 SD, not a single number.

**(iii) Standard deviation:** root mean square deviation from the mean. Uses all observations. Unit of distance on the normal curve. Sample formula uses n − 1.

**(iv) Coefficient of variation:** (SD / mean) × 100. Dimensionless. The correct way to compare variability of systolic pressure in children versus adults, or of weight versus height.
[/ANS]
