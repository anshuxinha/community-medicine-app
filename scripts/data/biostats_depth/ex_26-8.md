[EX]29. Write short notes on: (iii) random sample; (iv) cluster sample; (v) double-blind trial; (vi) biased sample.[/EX]
[ANS]
**(iii) Random sample**
A probability sample in which every unit of the defined population has a known (often equal) chance of selection. Drawn by lottery, random number tables, or a computer. Removes the investigator's choice and supports inference from sample to population. Simple random sampling suits a small homogeneous list; other random schemes (stratified, systematic, multistage) keep the chance mechanism while fitting field constraints.

**(iv) Cluster sample**
The sampling unit is a group (village, school, street). Clusters are drawn at random, then all or a sample of persons inside them are studied. It is cheaper when there is no complete name-list. People in one cluster are alike, so a design effect is applied and n must be larger than for simple random sampling. Classic use: World Health Organization 30 × 7 immunization coverage survey (30 clusters, 7 children aged 12–23 months).

**(v) Double-blind trial**
Neither the participant nor the bedside observer knows who receives active treatment and who receives placebo (or the comparison drug). Packs are coded A and B by the principal investigator. It removes subjective bias when the outcome is pain, sleep, or mood. Distinct from single-blind, where only the patient is masked.

**(vi) Biased sample**
A sample that systematically misrepresents the population: volunteers only, a hospital series used as if it were the community, a non-random clinic day, heavy non-response, or leading questions. Statistics computed from it do not estimate population parameters honestly. Random selection, a clear frame, and high response are the remedies.
[/ANS]
