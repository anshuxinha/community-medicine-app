[EX]29. Write short notes on: (i) normal distribution; (ii) normal curve.[/EX]
[ANS]
**(i) Normal distribution**
A large random sample of a continuous biological variable (height, pulse, birth weight), grouped in small class intervals, piles up around the mean and falls off symmetrically. About 68 percent of values lie within mean ± 1 SD, 95 percent within mean ± 1.96 SD, and 99.7 percent within mean ± 3 SD. Values beyond 2 SD are uncommon; beyond 3 SD are rare. This arithmetic description is the normal (Gaussian) distribution. It is the reference for Z, confidence limits, and many tests of significance.

**(ii) Normal curve**
The graph of that distribution is a smooth, bell-shaped, symmetrical curve. Mean, median, and mode coincide. Tails approach the baseline but never meet it. Points of inflection stand one SD from the mean on each side. Total area is 1. The **standard** normal curve is the special case with mean 0 and SD 1, obtained by Z = (X − μ) / σ. An ordinary height curve with mean 160 cm and SD 5 cm is still a normal curve; it is not "mean 0, SD 1" until it is standardized.

**Use.** The curve converts a raw measurement into a probability of being usual, which range and mean alone cannot do.
[/ANS]
