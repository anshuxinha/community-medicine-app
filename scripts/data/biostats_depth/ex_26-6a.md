[EX]36. Determine if height differs with sex. Boys: n 169, mean 168 cm, SD 14. Girls: n 54, mean 153 cm, SD 8.[/EX]
[ANS]
H0: μB = μG.
SE = √(14²/169 + 8²/54) = √(1.160 + 1.185) = 1.53
Z = (168 − 153) / 1.53 = **9.8**, p < 0.001.
Reject H0. Mean height differs highly significantly with sex.
[/ANS]

[EX]37. Serum protein: males n 18, mean 7.21, SD 0.26; females n 7, mean 6.90, SD 1.28. Is the female mean lower?[/EX]
[ANS]
Pooled two-sample t (df = 18 + 7 − 2 = 23).
s_p² = [17 × 0.26² + 6 × 1.28²] / 23 = 0.477
s_p = 0.691
SE = 0.691 × √(1/18 + 1/7) = 0.308
t = (7.21 − 6.90) / 0.308 = **1.01** (or −1.01 if female minus male).
|t| = 1.01, p > 0.10.
The apparent female deficit is **not significant**. Do not conclude that serum protein is lower in females from these data.
[/ANS]

[EX]45. Define the t-test and give situations for unpaired and paired t.[/EX]
[ANS]
Student's t compares a sample mean (or a mean difference) with a hypothesized value when σ is unknown and n is small or moderate. t = (estimate − H0 value) / SE, with df = n − 1 (one sample or paired) or n1 + n2 − 2 (two samples).

**Unpaired (independent) t:** two separate groups (normal versus malnourished chest circumference; boys versus girls; drug versus control animals).

**Paired t:** two readings on the same (or matched) subjects (blood pressure before and after a drug; haemoglobin before and after vitamin B12; breathing capacity before and after relief of ascites). Analyse the differences.
[/ANS]
