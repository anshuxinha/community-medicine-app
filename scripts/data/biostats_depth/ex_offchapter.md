[EX]97. What are the sources for collection of vital statistics? Discuss their utility in Community Medicine and public health administration.[/EX]
[ANS]
**Sources**
1. Population census (decennial full count; last completed Census of India 2011).
2. Civil Registration System (compulsory births and deaths under the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969).
3. Sample Registration System (dual record: continuous enumerator plus independent half-yearly survey).
4. Records of health departments and institutions (hospitals, notifiable diseases, programme reports).
5. Special surveys (National Family Health Survey and similar).
6. Periodic publications of the World Health Organization, the Registrar General of India, and the Directorate General of Health Services.
7. Other agencies and private establishments.

**Utility**
They supply denominators and numerators for birth, death, infant, and maternal rates; show whether a population is growing, aging, or ailing; identify leading causes of death; give early warning of outbreaks; and tell the administrator which programme to fund. Without them, health planning is sailing without a compass. Limitations (incomplete registration, hospital bias, under-notification) must be stated in any long answer.
[/ANS]

[EX]98. Census population of a city on 1 March 1971 was 3,38,750 and on 1 March 1981 was 4,02,565. Calculate the mid-year (1 July) expected population for 1973 and 1984 by the arithmetic-progression (AP) method.[/EX]
[ANS]
10-year increment = 4,02,565 − 3,38,750 = 63,815
Annual increment = 6,381.5 persons per year.

1 July is 4 months (1/3 year) after 1 March, so elapsed years from 1 March 1971:
- 1 July 1973: 2 + 1/3 = 2.333 years → 3,38,750 + 6,381.5 × 2.333 = **3,53,640**
- 1 July 1984: 13 + 1/3 = 13.333 years → 3,38,750 + 6,381.5 × 13.333 = **4,23,837**

(Printed key matches both figures.)
[/ANS]

[EX]100. Differentiate: (a) crude and specific vital rates; (b) birth and fertility rates; (c) incidence and prevalence; (d) crude and standardised death rates; (e) perinatal and neonatal mortality rates.[/EX]
[ANS]
**(a)** Crude rate uses the whole mid-year population as denominator (crude birth rate, crude death rate). Specific rate restricts numerator and denominator to one class (age-specific death rate, sex-specific rate).

**(b)** Birth rate (CBR) = live births × 1,000 / mid-year population. Fertility rates restrict the denominator to women of reproductive age (general fertility rate) or to one age band (age-specific fertility rate); total fertility rate sums the age-specific rates.

**(c)** Incidence = new cases in a period / population at risk. Prevalence = all existing cases at a point (or period) / population. Incidence measures flow; prevalence measures stock.

**(d)** Crude death rate is deaths × 1,000 / mid-year population and is distorted by age structure. A standardised death rate applies age-specific rates to a standard population so two places can be compared.

**(e)** Neonatal mortality: deaths in the first 28 days × 1,000 / live births. Perinatal mortality: (stillbirths + early neonatal deaths, usually first 7 days) × 1,000 / (live births + stillbirths). Perinatal includes stillbirths; neonatal does not.

(See also the Mortality indicators leaf for worked rate examples.)
[/ANS]

[EX]99. How do ratio, proportion, and rate differ in expressing vital indices? Give familiar examples.[/EX]
[ANS]
- **Ratio:** one number divided by another; the two need not come from the same whole. Example: sex ratio 933 females per 1,000 males; doctor–population ratio.
- **Proportion:** a ratio in which the numerator is **part of** the denominator. It is a fraction of a whole, often as a percent. Example: 40 of 200 white cells are lymphocytes = 0.20 (20 percent).
- **Rate:** a proportion with a **stated time** (and usually a multiplier such as 1,000). Example: crude death rate = deaths in a year × 1,000 / mid-year population; incidence rate of measles.

A case-fatality "rate" is strictly a proportion (deaths among cases, no separate time unit beyond the episode). Examiners still accept the traditional name.
[/ANS]

[EX]101. Mid-year population 20,52,000. Cholera attacks 228, deaths 57. Calculate the morbidity rate and the case-fatality rate.[/EX]
[ANS]
Morbidity (attack) rate = 228 / 20,52,000 × 1,000 = **0.11 per 1,000**
Case-fatality rate = 57 / 228 × 100 = **25 percent**

(Printed key: 0.11 and 25 percent.)
[/ANS]

[EX]102. Mid-year population 5,00,000. Live births 15,000, deaths 7,000, infant deaths 1,875. Calculate crude birth rate, crude death rate, and infant mortality rate.[/EX]
[ANS]
CBR = 15,000 / 5,00,000 × 1,000 = **30 per 1,000**
CDR = 7,000 / 5,00,000 × 1,000 = **14 per 1,000**
IMR = 1,875 / 15,000 × 1,000 = **125 per 1,000 live births**

[EX]103. Mid-year population 2,00,000. Live births 6,050, stillbirths 110, deaths in the first week 250, in the first month 480, in the first year 750. Calculate stillbirth, perinatal, neonatal, and infant mortality rates.[/EX]
[ANS]
Stillbirth rate = 110 / (6,050 + 110) × 1,000 = **17.86 per 1,000 total births**
Neonatal mortality rate = 480 / 6,050 × 1,000 = **79.3 per 1,000 live births**
Infant mortality rate = 750 / 6,050 × 1,000 = **124.0 per 1,000 live births**
Perinatal mortality (standard): (110 + 250) / (6,050 + 110) × 1,000 = **58.4 per 1,000 total births**

(Printed key: SBR 17.86, NMR 79.33, IMR 123.96, PMR 40.58. Use the standard perinatal definition above unless the paper specifies another early-neonatal cut. Compare historically with 1985 national levels as a comment, not as current India figures.)
[/ANS]

SOLVED EXERCISES

[EX]104. Explain indices used to monitor progress in a family planning programme.[/EX]
[ANS]
High-yield indices:
- **Couple protection rate:** percent of eligible couples effectively protected by any method.
- **Pearl index:** accidental pregnancies per 100 woman-years of exposure.
- **Life-table (multiple-decrement) failure rate:** month-by-month failure allowing for discontinuation; preferred for comparing methods.
- **Birth rate / general fertility rate / total fertility rate:** population-level impact.
- **Age at marriage, spacing, and terminal-method share:** mix and quality of the programme.
- **Unmet need:** women who want to space or limit but use no method.

State numerator, denominator, and one limitation for each in a long answer. Pearl assumes constant risk; the life table does not.
[/ANS]

[EX]105. Calculate the number of children produced per woman per year at the general fertility rates given in the source table (historical worked example).[/EX]
[ANS]
The printed exercise refers to a fertility table in the demography chapter. The method is:

Children per woman per year = (sum over age groups of [ASFR × number of women in that group]) / total women 15–49
or, equivalently, GFR / 1,000 if GFR is already live births per 1,000 women 15–49 per year.

**Printed numerical key: 0.067 children per woman per year.**
That is a general fertility rate of 67 per 1,000 women of reproductive age. It is a historical classroom figure, not a current national estimate.
[/ANS]
