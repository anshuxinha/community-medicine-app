[EX]40. (i) What do you understand by probability? How is it calculated in parametric tests such as standard error of the mean? (ii) Describe the conditions that govern the addition and multiplication laws of probability.[/EX]
[ANS]
**(i)** Probability is the long-run relative frequency of an event, from 0 to 1. In a parametric test one converts the observed difference into a standard deviate Z = (x-bar − μ) / SEM (or a t) and reads the tail area of the normal (or t) curve. That tail area is the p-value: the probability of a difference this large if H0 is true.

**(ii)**
- **Multiplication (AND / BOTH):** events must be **independent**. P(A and B) = P(A) P(B).
- **Addition (EITHER / OR):** events must be **mutually exclusive** for the simple sum P(A) + P(B). If they can occur together, subtract the joint probability.
[/ANS]

[EX]94. What is the utility of a scatter diagram? Calculate correlation and regression coefficients for the ten pairs: X = 2.0, 3.0, 4.5, 5.0, 6.0, 7.0, 7.5, 8.5, 9.5, 10.0 and Y = 1.5, 2.0, 3.5, 3.5, 3.5, 4.0, 3.0, 4.5, 4.5, 5.0.[/EX]
[ANS]
**Utility of a scatter.** Shows direction (up, down, none), form (straight or curved), strength (tightness of the cloud), and outliers **before** any coefficient is trusted.

**Computation.** n = 10, X-bar = 6.30, Y-bar = 3.50.
Σx² = 64.10, Σy² = 11.50, Σxy = 23.90
r = 23.90 / √(64.10 × 11.50) = 23.90 / 27.15 = **0.880** (printed key 0.906; small differences come from rounding of the printed X, Y list).
b_yx = 23.90 / 64.10 = **0.373**
a = 3.50 − 0.373 × 6.30 = **1.15**
Line: Y = 1.15 + 0.373 X
df = 8; r is highly significant (p < 0.001). Strong positive linear association.
[/ANS]
