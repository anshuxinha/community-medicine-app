OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This section is the statute book for reproductive choice, safe abortion, and prohibition of sex selection. Clinical methods of Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) stay in the family planning chapter. A full answer names year, object, grounds, who may terminate, at what gestation, where, and which board sits beyond twenty-four weeks. The same paper often pairs MTP with the Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (Prohibition of Sex Selection) Act, 1994 (PCPNDT Act), then asks a short note on the Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021 and the Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (ART Act).

DEFINITIONS
REGISTERED MEDICAL PRACTITIONER (RMP): A medical practitioner who possesses a recognised medical qualification, whose name has been entered in a State Medical Register, and who has such experience or training in gynaecology and obstetrics as prescribed by the MTP Rules.

PRENATAL DIAGNOSTIC TECHNIQUE: Ultrasound, foetoscopy, sampling of amniotic fluid, chorionic villi, blood, or any other tissue or fluid of a pregnant woman, or any product of conception, for antenatal diagnosis of abnormalities or sex of the foetus.

ALTRUISTIC SURROGACY: Surrogacy in which no charges, expenses, fees, remuneration, or monetary incentive of whatever nature, except the medical expenses and such other prescribed costs, are given to the surrogate mother or her dependents or her representative.

[SN]MTP Act[/SN]

[LAQ]Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act[/LAQ]

MEDICAL TERMINATION OF PREGNANCY ACT, 1971
The Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971 liberalises and regulates termination of certain pregnancies by RMPs. It came into force on 1 April 1972. The Medical Termination of Pregnancy (Amendment) Act, 2021 (introduced as the Amendment Bill, 2020, and passed by the Rajya Sabha on 16 March 2021) raised gestational limits, extended contraceptive-failure protection to any woman and her partner, created a State Medical Board route beyond twenty-four weeks, and added a privacy offence. Do not write the obsolete opinion rule (one RMP up to twelve weeks and two RMPs from twelve to twenty weeks). Current law is one RMP up to twenty weeks.

OBJECT
To provide for termination of certain pregnancies by RMPs, and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto: safe, legal abortion on stated grounds, in an approved place, by a trained doctor, with confidentiality.

GROUNDS (THE CLASSIC FIVE)
A pregnancy may be terminated when the RMP (or RMPs) is of the opinion, formed in good faith, that:

1. Therapeutic: continuance would involve a risk to the life of the pregnant woman, or of grave injury to her physical or mental health.
2. Eugenic: there is a substantial risk that if the child were born, it would suffer from such physical or mental abnormalities as to be seriously handicapped.
3. Humanitarian: the pregnancy is alleged to have been caused by rape. The anguish is presumed to constitute a grave injury to mental health.
4. Failure of contraception: the pregnancy results from failure of any device or method used by any woman or her partner for limiting the number of children or preventing pregnancy. Grave injury to mental health is presumed. The 2021 Amendment extends this ground to any woman and her partner, not only a married woman and her husband.
5. Socio-economic: in determining risk of injury to health, account may be taken of the pregnant woman's actual or reasonably foreseeable environment.

**Mnemonic:** TEHFS
- T: Therapeutic (life or grave injury to health)
- E: Eugenic (serious handicap in the child)
- H: Humanitarian (rape)
- F: Failure of contraception (mental-health injury presumed)
- S: Socio-economic (actual or reasonably foreseeable environment)

GESTATION AND OPINION (CURRENT RULE)

| Gestation | Who must opine | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 20 weeks | One RMP | Any statutory ground |
| More than 20 weeks and up to 24 weeks | Two RMPs | Only the categories prescribed in the Medical Termination of Pregnancy (Amendment) Rules, 2021, Rule 3B |
| Beyond 24 weeks | State-level Medical Board | Substantial foetal abnormalities |
| Any gestation | One RMP | Immediately necessary to save the life of the pregnant woman (section 5) |

RULE 3B CATEGORIES (20 TO 24 WEEKS)
Two RMPs may terminate between twenty and twenty-four weeks for:

1. Survivors of sexual assault, rape, or incest.
2. Minors.
3. Change of marital status during the ongoing pregnancy (widowhood or divorce).
4. Women with physical disabilities (major disability as per the criteria laid down under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016).
5. Mentally ill women, including mental retardation.
6. Foetal malformation that has a substantial risk of being incompatible with life, or if the child is born it may suffer from such physical or mental abnormalities as to be seriously handicapped.
7. Women in humanitarian settings or disaster or emergency situations as declared by the Government.

**Mnemonic:** SAM-WMF-H
- S: Survivors of sexual assault, rape, or incest
- A: Age: minors
- M: Marital change (widowhood or divorce)
- W: Women with major physical disabilities
- M: Mentally ill women, including mental retardation
- F: Foetal malformation with the statutory risk
- H: Humanitarian, disaster, or emergency settings

MEDICAL BOARD
For termination beyond twenty-four weeks on the ground of substantial foetal abnormalities, the State Government constitutes a Medical Board. Composition: a gynaecologist, a paediatrician, a radiologist or sonologist, and such other members as the State Government may notify. The Board examines the woman and the reports, and permits or denies termination.

PLACE
Termination may be done only in a hospital established or maintained by the Government, or a place approved for the purpose by the Government or a District Level Committee constituted by that Government. Emergency life-saving termination under section 5 is the recognised exception when the place rule would cost a life.

PRIVACY
An RMP who has terminated a pregnancy shall not reveal the name and other particulars of the woman except to a person authorised by any law for the time being in force. Contravention is punishable with imprisonment which may extend to one year, or with fine, or with both.

NATIONAL CONTEXT
Unsafe abortion is a preventable cause of maternal death. The 1971 Act took abortion out of the criminal code for listed indications and put it into a licensed medical service. The 2021 Amendment is the examination update: twenty weeks with one opinion, twenty-four weeks for Rule 3B categories, Medical Board after twenty-four weeks, contraceptive failure for any woman and her partner, and a privacy offence. The public-health companion statute is the PCPNDT Act, 1994, which stops the same technologies from being used for sex selection.

CONCLUSION
Write MTP as a rights-and-safety statute, not as a list of surgical methods. Open with 1971, in force 1 April 1972, Amendment 2021. Draw the gestation table. Recite TEHFS and Rule 3B. Close with place, privacy (up to one year and/or fine), and the Medical Board.

> **EXAM TIP:** Never write one doctor up to twelve weeks. Current law is one RMP to twenty weeks, two RMPs from twenty to twenty-four weeks only for Rule 3B, Medical Board after twenty-four weeks for substantial foetal abnormality, and one RMP at any time to save life.

[SN]PCPNDT Act[/SN]

PRE-CONCEPTION AND PRE-NATAL DIAGNOSTIC TECHNIQUES (PROHIBITION OF SEX SELECTION) ACT, 1994
The PCPNDT Act, 1994 (originally the Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques Act, 1994; the 2003 Amendment added pre-conception techniques and the words “Prohibition of Sex Selection”) is the statute against female foeticide and sex selection. Object: to prohibit sex selection, before or after conception, and to regulate prenatal diagnostic techniques for the purpose of detecting genetic or metabolic disorders, chromosomal abnormalities, certain congenital malformations, or sex-linked disorders, and for matters connected therewith.

OPERATIVE PROVISIONS
- Section 6: no person shall conduct or cause to be conducted any prenatal diagnostic technique including ultrasonography for the purpose of determining the sex of a foetus; no person shall, by whatever means, cause or allow to be caused selection of sex before or after conception.
- Every Genetic Counselling Centre, Genetic Laboratory, and Genetic Clinic must be registered. The register of these units is a standard viva item.
- Form F must be filled for every diagnostic procedure on a pregnant woman (indication, consent, declaration that sex will not be communicated).
- Written consent of the pregnant woman is required before using a prenatal diagnostic technique. The sex of the foetus shall not be communicated to her or to her relatives by words, signs, or in any other manner.
- Advertisement of facilities for sex selection or for determination of sex is banned.
- Appropriate Authority (commonly the district Appropriate Authority: Civil Surgeon or Chief Medical Officer in most States) inspects, searches, seizes records and machines, and suspends or cancels registration. An Advisory Committee assists the Appropriate Authority. The Supreme Court has directed enforcement; a National Inspection and Monitoring Committee is often named in viva.
- Records shall be preserved as prescribed, commonly taught as two years of records plus until disposal of any proceeding.
- Offences attract imprisonment and fine. The registered unit, the owner, and the person conducting the technique can be prosecuted. Courts have treated the Act as a social-welfare statute and have been strict on incomplete Form F.

INDIA HOOK
Declining child sex ratio is the epidemiological reason this Act exists. MTP legalises abortion on health and humanitarian grounds. PCPNDT forbids using diagnosis to choose a male child. An answer that mixes the two statutes loses marks. Name Form F, section 6, the district Appropriate Authority, and the advertisement ban.

> **EXAM TIP:** Open with the full title and the 2003 pre-conception amendment. Then section 6, Form F, registration of the three types of unit, district Appropriate Authority plus Advisory Committee, advertisement ban, and records as prescribed (commonly two years plus till disposal of proceedings).

[SN]Surrogacy Regulation Act[/SN]

SURROGACY (REGULATION) ACT, 2021
The Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021 permits only altruistic surrogacy and prohibits commercial surrogacy. It sits with the ART Act, 2021. National and State Assisted Reproductive Technology and Surrogacy Boards advise on policy, and appropriate authorities register and inspect surrogacy clinics.

HIGH-YIELD PROVISIONS
- Commercial surrogacy, abandonment of the surrogate child, exploitation of the surrogate mother, and sale or import of human embryos or gametes for surrogacy are prohibited.
- An intending couple must be married, of the prescribed age (commonly taught as twenty-three to fifty years for the woman and twenty-six to fifty-five years for the man), and must have a medical indication of proven infertility as certified.
- The surrogate mother must be a married woman with a child of her own, aged twenty-five to thirty-five years, and may act as a surrogate only once in her lifetime.
- Surrogacy clinics must be registered. Sex selection in any form is forbidden.
- A surrogacy procedure requires prescribed consents and the order of a Magistrate for parentage and custody as laid down in the Act and Rules.

Keep the note short. Do not invent compensation figures or clinic counts.

> **EXAM TIP:** Three lines win this short note: altruistic only (no commercial), intending couple (married, age bands, proven infertility), surrogate (married, own child, 25-35 years, once in a lifetime), plus National and State ART and Surrogacy Boards.

[SN]ART Act[/SN]

ASSISTED REPRODUCTIVE TECHNOLOGY (REGULATION) ACT, 2021
The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 regulates clinics and banks that provide ART. Every clinic and bank must register. A National Registry of clinics and banks is maintained. The woman undergoing ART is commonly taught as being within twenty-one to fifty years of age. Sex selection is prohibited. Donors must be screened as prescribed. The Act works with the Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021 through the same Board architecture.

> **EXAM TIP:** Registration of clinics and banks, National Registry, age band of the woman (commonly 21-50 years), donor screening, and a flat ban on sex selection are enough for the short note.
