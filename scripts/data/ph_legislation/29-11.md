OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This section groups four statutes examiners set together after professional regulation: organ and tissue transplantation, blood-centre licensing, maintenance of older persons, and the ban on manual scavenging. Clinical transplant technique and blood-bank bench work stay in their own chapters. Here the answer is object, definitions, authorities, and the public-health reason each Act exists.

DEFINITIONS
BRAIN-STEM DEATH: The stage at which all functions of the brain-stem have permanently and irreversibly ceased, certified in the manner prescribed by a Board of medical experts.

NEAR RELATIVE (TRANSPLANTATION): Spouse, son, daughter, father, mother, brother, sister, grandfather, grandmother, grandson, or granddaughter, as defined in the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994 as amended.

AUTHORISATION COMMITTEE: Committee notified under the Act to approve living donation where the donor is not a near relative, and other specified donations including swap donation.

SENIOR CITIZEN: A person who has attained the age of sixty years or above, as defined in the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007.

MANUAL SCAVENGER: A person engaged or employed for manually cleaning, carrying, disposing of, or handling human excreta in an insanitary latrine or in an open drain or pit, as defined in the 2013 Act.

INSANITARY LATRINE: A latrine that requires human excreta to be cleaned or handled manually, before it is fully decomposed.

[SN]Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994[/SN]

[LAQ]Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act: provisions and public health importance[/LAQ]

TRANSPLANTATION OF HUMAN ORGANS AND TISSUES ACT, 1994
The Transplantation of Human Organs Act, 1994 was amended in 2011 to include tissues, and is now cited as the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994 (THOTA). Object: to regulate removal, storage, and transplantation of human organs and tissues for therapeutic purposes, and to prevent commercial dealings.

The Act defines brain-stem death and permits organ retrieval from a person so certified. Certification is by a Board of medical experts as prescribed in the Rules (the treating practitioner, hospital administration, and a neurologist or neurosurgeon among the prescribed members).

Living donation is first directed to a near relative. Near relatives listed in the Act, as amended, are spouse, son, daughter, father, mother, brother, sister, grandparent, and grandchild.

**Mnemonic:** SCSPG (Spouse; Children; Siblings; Parents; Grandparent and grandchild)

If the donor is not a near relative, the donation requires prior approval of the Authorisation Committee. After the 2011 amendment, swap (paired) donation is permitted in specified circumstances: two near-relative pairs may exchange donors when each donor is incompatible with the intended recipient in that pair but compatible with the other recipient, subject to Authorisation Committee approval.

The organisational network is the National Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisation (NOTTO), Regional Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisations (ROTTO), and State Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisations (SOTTO). Transplant hospitals must be registered. An Appropriate Authority inspects and can suspend or cancel registration. Removal, storage, or transplantation for payment, and advertisement of organs or tissues for sale, are offences. The 2011 amendment enhanced imprisonment and fine.

NATIONAL BLOOD POLICY AND DRUGS AND COSMETICS RULES (ONE BOX)
Blood is a drug. Blood centres (blood banks) are licensed under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules framed under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940. The National Blood Policy, 2002 of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare sets the policy frame: an adequate supply of safe blood from voluntary, non-remunerated donors, screened and stored to standard. Technical standards of the National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO) and of the National Blood Transfusion Council apply. Paid professional donation is contrary to policy. Do not expand this box into a second blood-bank chapter.

> **EXAM TIP:** 1994, tissues added in 2011. Brain-stem death certified by a prescribed Board, SCSPG near relatives, Authorisation Committee for non-near-relative living donation, swap after 2011, NOTTO-ROTTO-SOTTO, enhanced punishment for commercial dealing. One box: blood-centre licence under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules; National Blood Policy 2002 (voluntary, non-remunerated).

[SN]Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007[/SN]

MAINTENANCE AND WELFARE OF PARENTS AND SENIOR CITIZENS ACT, 2007
Object: maintenance and welfare of parents and senior citizens. Senior citizen means a person aged sixty years and above. Parent includes a biological, adoptive, or step parent, whether or not that parent is a senior citizen. Children and specified heirs have a duty to maintain the parent or senior citizen (food, clothing, residence, and medical attendance and treatment). Application lies before a Maintenance Tribunal (commonly the Sub-Divisional Magistrate as prescribed). An Appellate Tribunal hears appeals. The State Government shall, as far as possible, establish and maintain old-age homes, in a phased manner, in each district. Do not invent a bed count. The Act provides for protection of life and property of senior citizens, including a procedure against abandonment, and for voiding a transfer of property if the transferee fails to provide the promised maintenance. Amendment proposals have been circulated. They are not to be written as enacted law.

> **EXAM TIP:** Sixty years; children and heirs must maintain; Maintenance Tribunal; old-age homes as far as possible; protection of life and property. One line that bills to amend the Act are not yet law.

[SN]Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013[/SN]

PROHIBITION OF EMPLOYMENT AS MANUAL SCAVENGERS AND THEIR REHABILITATION ACT, 2013
This Act replaced the Employment of Manual Scavengers and Construction of Dry Latrines (Prohibition) Act, 1993. Object: to prohibit employment of manual scavengers, the construction or continuance of insanitary latrines, and hazardous cleaning of sewers and septic tanks, and to provide for identification and rehabilitation of persons so employed.

No person, local authority, or agency shall construct, maintain, or use an insanitary latrine, or engage or employ a manual scavenger. Local authorities must survey, identify insanitary latrines and manual scavengers, demolish or convert insanitary latrines, and include identified persons in a rehabilitation scheme (housing, training, livelihood, and related entitlements as prescribed). Hazardous cleaning of a sewer or septic tank without prescribed protective gear and safety devices is prohibited. Contravention is an offence (imprisonment and fine as laid down). Offences are cognizable.

Manual scavenging is a faecal-oral and occupational hazard (human waste, confined spaces, sewer gases) layered on denial of dignity. The 2013 Act is both a sanitation statute and a labour-and-dignity statute. Programme language on toilets is not a substitute for naming this Act.

> **EXAM TIP:** 2013 replaced 1993. Ban insanitary latrines and manual scavenging, identify and rehabilitate, offence. Close with faecal-oral disease, occupational hazard, and dignity.
