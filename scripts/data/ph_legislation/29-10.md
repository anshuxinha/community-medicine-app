OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This leaf is the legal home of vital events and of the population count. Demography already taught the rates. Write the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969 (RBD Act), the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Act, 2023, and the Census Act, 1948. The Civil Registration System (CRS) is the continuous legal system under the RBD Act. The Sample Registration System (SRS) is a dual-record demographic survey, not this Act; its field methods stay in the demography chapter. Census is the denominator for almost every health indicator.

DEFINITIONS
VITAL STATISTICS: Collection, compilation, analysis, and presentation of data on vital events (live births, deaths, stillbirths) for legal identity and public-health use.

CIVIL REGISTRATION SYSTEM: Compulsory, continuous, permanent recording of vital events under the RBD Act and State Rules.

MEDICAL CERTIFICATION OF CAUSE OF DEATH (MCCD): Certification of the cause of death by a registered medical practitioner on the prescribed form, coded to the International Classification of Diseases (ICD).

STILLBIRTH: A foetal death, as defined for registration, that must be reported and registered. It is not a live birth and does not enter the live-birth denominator.

CENSUS: Collecting, compiling, and publishing demographic, economic, and social data pertaining, at a specified time, to all persons in a country, taken in India under the Census Act, 1948.

[SN]Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969[/SN]

[LAQ]Registration of births and deaths in India[/LAQ]

REGISTRATION OF BIRTHS AND DEATHS ACT, 1969
The RBD Act, 1969 provides for compulsory registration of births and deaths and for compilation of vital statistics. The subject is on the Concurrent List. Parliament enacted a uniform Central law; States frame Rules and run the field machinery.

Hierarchy:
- Registrar General of India at the Centre (Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner).
- Chief Registrar of Births and Deaths in each State or Union Territory.
- District Registrar.
- Local Registrar for a local area (municipality, panchayat, or other notified unit).

**Mnemonic:** RCDL (Registrar General, Chief Registrar, District Registrar, Local Registrar)

Births are to be registered within twenty-one days (classic teaching). Deaths similarly. The Act requires reporting within such time as may be prescribed; State Rules have long taught the twenty-one-day window. Delayed registration is allowed under the Act and Rules with late fee, written permission, or a magistrate's order according to how late the report is. A further 2026 amendment bill on delayed registration has been introduced. It is not to be written as enacted law.

Persons required to report are prescribed: the medical officer in charge when the event occurs in a hospital, the head of the household for a domiciliary event, and other listed informants (including, after 2023, specified persons in adoption, surrogacy, and single-parent births). Registration is at the place of occurrence. Hospitals and attending practitioners must certify the cause of death on the MCCD form using ICD. Stillbirths are registrable events.

A birth certificate is the child's first legal identity. CRS is the only legal continuous count of births and deaths. Incomplete CRS is why India still leans on SRS for national mortality rates.

> **EXAM TIP:** Draw RCDL. Compulsory registration, twenty-one days (classic teaching) for births and deaths, MCCD with ICD, stillbirth. Close with CRS as the legal continuous system under this Act, not a survey.

[SN]Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Act, 2023[/SN]

REGISTRATION OF BIRTHS AND DEATHS (AMENDMENT) ACT, 2023
The 2023 Amendment modernises the 1969 Act. It does not repeal it. The Registrar General of India shall maintain a national database of registered births and deaths. Chief Registrars and Registrars must share registered-event data with that database. The Chief Registrar maintains a corresponding State database. The national database may be made available to authorities preparing other notified databases (including, as provided, population register, electoral rolls, and ration cards) with Central Government approval. A person may obtain a birth or death certificate electronically or otherwise.

The amendment provides for collection of Aadhaar numbers of the parents and the informant in the case of births, and corresponding collection as provided for deaths, from the persons required to report the event.

For persons born on or after the amendment takes effect, the birth certificate is to be used as proof of date and place of birth for specified purposes:

1. Admission to an educational institution.
2. Issuance of a driving licence.
3. Preparation of a voter list.
4. Registration of a marriage.
5. Appointment to a government post.
6. Issuance of a passport.
7. Issuance of an Aadhaar number.
8. Any other purpose as may be determined by the Central Government.

**Mnemonic:** ADV-MAPAO (Admission, Driving licence, Voter list, Marriage, Appointment, Passport, Aadhaar, Other)

An appeal against an order of the Registrar or District Registrar lies as provided, within the time laid down in the amendment.

> **EXAM TIP:** Three 2023 points: national database with mandatory sharing, digital certificates, and the birth certificate as a single document (ADV-MAPAO). Add Aadhaar collection as provided. Do not treat any 2026 delayed-registration bill as current law.

[SN]Census Act, 1948[/SN]

CENSUS ACT, 1948
The Census Act, 1948 (with Census Rules) is the statute for India's periodic population census. The Census Commissioner of India directs the operations. Directors or Superintendents of Census Operations, and appointed census officers, carry out houselisting and housing enumeration, then population enumeration of every person.

Individual census returns are confidential. They shall not be used as evidence except a prosecution under the Census Act itself. Census supplies the population denominator for almost every health rate: birth and death rates, infant mortality (when live births come from CRS or SRS), coverage, and manpower ratios. Houselisting also yields housing, water, and sanitation variables. Until a later round is published, examination answers still treat the 2011 Census as the last completed decennial count.

Keep three sources distinct. CRS is the legal, continuous, universal system under the RBD Act. SRS is a dual-record demographic survey used while CRS remains incomplete; it is not a creature of the RBD Act or of the Census Act. Census is the periodic total count and the usual denominator. Do not copy SRS field methods into this leaf.

> **EXAM TIP:** Census Act 1948, Census Commissioner, houselisting then population enumeration, confidentiality of individual returns, denominator for health rates. One line: CRS is the RBD Act system; SRS is a survey, not this Act.
