OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This section covers outbreak powers inside India and India’s duties under the International Health Regulations (IHR), 2005. Clinical epidemic response, triage, and the disaster-management cycle stay in the disaster and epidemiology chapters. International agencies stay in the International Health chapter. The answer is statutes, authorities, and notification duties.

A resident who names only “lockdown” without the parent Act will fail a viva. Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) national response used the Disaster Management Act, 2005 together with the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897.

DEFINITIONS
DANGEROUS EPIDEMIC DISEASE: The phrase used in the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897. The Act does not define a list of diseases. The State Government decides that a dangerous epidemic disease has visited, or threatens, the State or any part of it, and that ordinary law is insufficient.

DISASTER (Disaster Management Act, 2005): A catastrophe, mishap, calamity or grave occurrence in any area, arising from natural or man-made causes, or by accident or negligence, which results in substantial loss of life or human suffering, or damage to and destruction of property, or damage to or degradation of the environment, and is of such a nature or magnitude as to be beyond the coping capacity of the community of the affected area.

PUBLIC HEALTH EMERGENCY OF INTERNATIONAL CONCERN (PHEIC): An extraordinary event which constitutes a public health risk to other States through the international spread of disease and which potentially requires a coordinated international response. Determined by the Director-General of the World Health Organization (WHO) under IHR 2005.

NATIONAL IHR FOCAL POINT: The national centre designated by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare for urgent communications with WHO under the IHR.

[SN]Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897[/SN]

EPIDEMIC DISEASES ACT, 1897
Colonial statute, still in force. Short, and deliberately wide.

When the State Government is satisfied that the State, or any part of it, is visited by or threatened with an outbreak of any dangerous epidemic disease, and that the ordinary provisions of the law are insufficient, it may take special measures and prescribe temporary regulations to be observed by the public.

Named power that examiners expect: inspection of persons travelling by railway or otherwise, and segregation in hospital, temporary accommodation or otherwise, of persons suspected by the inspecting officer of being infected.

The Central Government has corresponding power for ports of entry and departure, and for inter-State movement, when the threat crosses State lines.

Public-health purpose: give the executive a fast legal handle when ordinary municipal and police law cannot contain an epidemic.

EPIDEMIC DISEASES (AMENDMENT) ACT, 2020
Do not invent sections beyond what follows.

The 2020 Amendment created offences for violence against health-care service personnel, and for damage to property, during an epidemic.
- The offence is cognizable and non-bailable.
- Imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years, and fine from Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh.
- If the act causes grievous hurt: imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years, and a higher fine.
- The court may also award compensation for injury and for damage to property.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not write that the 1897 Act was repealed by the 2005 Act. Both remain available.
- Do not invent a national lockdown section inside the 1897 Act. COVID-19 lockdown and national guidelines were issued under the Disaster Management Act, 2005.
- Do not add 2020 clauses that are not on violence, property damage, cognizable and non-bailable character, the prison terms and fines above, or compensation.

> **EXAM TIP:** Open with “colonial statute, still in force.” Then: State Government, dangerous epidemic disease, special measures and regulations, inspection of travellers. Then the 2020 Amendment in four lines (violence and property; cognizable and non-bailable; 3 months to 5 years and Rs 50,000 to Rs 2 lakh; grievous hurt 6 months to 7 years plus compensation). Close that COVID-19 used this Act together with the Disaster Management Act, 2005.

[SN]Disaster Management Act, 2005[/SN]

DISASTER MANAGEMENT ACT, 2005
Object: to provide for the effective management of disasters and for matters connected with it, including mitigation, preparedness, response and recovery.

Institutional stack (draw this)
- National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA): chairperson is the Prime Minister.
- State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA): chairperson is the Chief Minister.
- District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA): the district-level authority.
- National Executive Committee: assists NDMA and issues guidelines and directions for implementation.

COVID-19: the national lockdown and the successive Ministry of Home Affairs guidelines were issued in exercise of Disaster Management Act powers, used together with State regulations under the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897.

Hospital disaster plan: every hospital is expected to keep a written disaster-management plan (mass casualty, surge, evacuation, and command).

Public-health purpose: a standing chain of command for events that exceed ordinary health-department powers, including epidemics that are notified as disasters.

**Mnemonic:** NSD-E
- N: National DMA (Prime Minister)
- S: State DMA (Chief Minister)
- D: District DMA
- E: National Executive Committee

> **EXAM TIP:** Draw NDMA (Prime Minister), SDMA (Chief Minister), DDMA, and the National Executive Committee. One line that the COVID-19 lockdown used this Act. One line on the hospital disaster plan.

INDIAN AIRCRAFT (PUBLIC HEALTH) RULES
These rules authorise health measures at airports: inspection of aircraft and passengers, isolation, disinfection, and checking of prescribed vaccination certificates. Yellow fever has been the classic examination example for airport public-health control in India. Write one sentence only unless the question names aircraft or yellow fever.

HOW TO WRITE THE LONG ANSWER
1. Definition of public-health emergency powers.
2. Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897 (special measures, inspection of travellers) plus the 2020 Amendment.
3. Disaster Management Act, 2005 (definition of disaster; NDMA, SDMA, DDMA; National Executive Committee).
4. One line: COVID-19 used both Acts.
5. One line on IHR 2005 and PHEIC (next section), so the answer is not only domestic.

> **EXAM TIP:** For a combined epidemic-and-disaster long answer, write 1897 then 2005 then “both used in COVID-19.” Draw the NDMA-SDMA-DDMA stack with the chairpersons. Do not call the 1897 Act obsolete.

[SN]International Health Regulations, 2005[/SN]

INTERNATIONAL HEALTH REGULATIONS, 2005
IHR 2005 is a binding international legal instrument under WHO. It binds Member States, including India. It is not an Indian Act. Examiners pair it with the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897 and the Disaster Management Act, 2005.

Object: prevent, protect against, control, and provide a public-health response to the international spread of disease, in ways that avoid unnecessary interference with international traffic and trade.

PHEIC: defined above. The WHO Director-General determines a PHEIC after advice from an Emergency Committee.

Core capacities: each State Party must develop, strengthen and maintain capacity to detect, assess, notify and report events, and to respond promptly and effectively.

Decision instrument (Annex 2): an algorithm the National IHR Focal Point uses to decide whether an event must be notified to WHO. It asks whether the event is serious, unusual or unexpected, whether there is a significant risk of international spread, and whether there is a significant risk of international travel or trade restriction.

Four diseases always notifiable in the older IHR teaching (and still listed as always-notifiable events in the decision instrument)
1. Smallpox.
2. Poliomyelitis due to wild poliovirus.
3. Human influenza caused by a new subtype.
4. Severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS).

Other events (cholera, yellow fever, viral haemorrhagic fevers, clusters of unknown disease, chemical or radionuclear events with international risk) are run through the decision instrument. Do not write that only four diseases are ever notified under current IHR.

National IHR Focal Point: designated by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. It is the 24-hour contact point for WHO.

This section is the legal skeleton only. Agency history, ICD, and recent outbreak narratives belong in the International Health chapter.

**Mnemonic:** SPIS
- S: Smallpox
- P: Poliomyelitis due to wild poliovirus
- I: Influenza caused by a new subtype
- S: SARS

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not treat IHR 2005 as a Central Act of Parliament.
- Do not write the old three-disease quarantine list (cholera, plague, yellow fever) as if it were still the whole of IHR.
- Do not duplicate the International Health chapter.

> **EXAM TIP:** Define IHR as binding WHO law, then PHEIC, core capacities, and the decision instrument. List the four always-notifiable diseases (SPIS). End with “National IHR Focal Point designated by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.” Pair the last sentence with the Epidemic Diseases Act and the Disaster Management Act so the examiner sees the domestic hook.
