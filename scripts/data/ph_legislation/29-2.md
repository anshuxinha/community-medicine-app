OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This leaf groups statutes that protect the child as a patient, a worker, a learner, and a dependent. The Medical Officer meets these Acts in the casualty (sexual offence), the labour inspection (under-fourteen worker), the anganwadi and nursery (feeding bottles and infant milk substitutes), and the school health programme (Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009). Write each Act by year, definition of the child, the duty it creates for a doctor or a manager, and the authority that enforces it.

DEFINITIONS
CHILD (PROTECTION OF CHILDREN FROM SEXUAL OFFENCES ACT, 2012): Any person below the age of eighteen years.

CHILD IN CONFLICT WITH LAW: A child who is alleged or found to have committed an offence, and who has not completed eighteen years of age on the date of commission of such offence.

CHILD IN NEED OF CARE AND PROTECTION: A child as defined in the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 who is found without means of subsistence, is orphaned, abandoned, or surrendered, is being or is likely to be abused or exploited, or falls in any other clause of that definition.

ADOLESCENT (CHILD AND ADOLESCENT LABOUR ACT): A person who has completed fourteenth year of age but has not completed eighteenth year.

INFANT MILK SUBSTITUTE: Any food being marketed or otherwise represented as a partial or total replacement for mother's milk, whether or not suitable for that purpose.

[SN]POCSO Act[/SN]

PROTECTION OF CHILDREN FROM SEXUAL OFFENCES ACT, 2012
The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 (POCSO Act) is a gender-neutral special law. A child is any person under eighteen years. It creates graded offences, a duty to report, Special Courts, and rules for medical examination and emergency care. For a Medical Officer this is the highest-yield child statute after the Juvenile Justice Act.

OFFENCES (LEARN THE NAMES)
- Penetrative sexual assault and aggravated penetrative sexual assault.
- Sexual assault and aggravated sexual assault.
- Sexual harassment.
- Use of a child for pornographic purposes, and storage or possession of child pornography as provided.

Aggravated forms include, among other statutory situations, offences by a person in authority or trust, offences in a shared household or institutional setting, and offences causing grievous hurt or committed on a child with disability. Do not invent tariff years unless the question names a section.

MANDATORY REPORTING (SECTION 19)
Any person who has apprehension that an offence under the Act is likely to be committed, or has knowledge that such an offence has been committed, shall provide information to the Special Juvenile Police Unit or the local police. Failure to report is itself an offence. A doctor who treats injuries consistent with sexual assault and stays silent commits that offence. The report is not a finding of guilt. It is a statutory communication.

MEDICAL EXAMINATION AND EMERGENCY CARE
- Examination is done with the consent of the child, or of a parent or competent person as provided, and as far as practicable by a woman doctor if the child is a female.
- Emergency medical care cannot be refused. The hospital shall provide first-aid, prophylaxis as indicated (including for human immunodeficiency virus exposure and pregnancy prevention where clinically indicated), and treatment of injuries, without waiting for a legal formality that would delay care.
- The child shall not be called repeatedly for medical examination. The report goes to the Investigating Officer and the Special Court.

SPECIAL COURTS
Each district is to have a Special Court (or a designated Court of Session) for speedy trial. In-camera proceedings, the assistance of a specialised interpreter or expert, and restrictions on aggressive cross-examination of the child are built into the scheme. Identity of the child is not to be disclosed in media.

INDIA HOOK
POCSO converts the casualty encounter into a legal duty. The viva question is: will you treat first, and will you report? The answers are yes and yes. Consent for examination is still required except where delay would cost life or health. Clinical forensic detail belongs in the forensic medicine answer; here name section 19, Special Courts, and the ban on refusing emergency care.

> **EXAM TIP:** Definition (under 18), the five offence heads, section 19 mandatory reporting (failure is an offence), Special Courts, consent for examination, and the rule that emergency care cannot be refused. That is a complete Medical Officer short note.

[SN]Juvenile Justice Act 2015[/SN]

JUVENILE JUSTICE (CARE AND PROTECTION OF CHILDREN) ACT, 2015
The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 (JJ Act, 2015) is the current code. It repeals the Juvenile Justice Act, 1986 and the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2000 for examination purposes. Write 2015 as the living statute. It has two streams: the Child in Conflict with Law, and the Child in Need of Care and Protection.

TWO STREAMS AND TWO AUTHORITIES

| Stream | Who | Forum |
|---|---|---|
| Child in Conflict with Law | Alleged or found to have committed an offence, under 18 on the date of the offence | Juvenile Justice Board (Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate of the First Class, with two social workers, at least one a woman) |
| Child in Need of Care and Protection | Abandoned, orphaned, abused, exploited, or otherwise within the statutory definition | Child Welfare Committee (a chairperson and four members, at least one a woman) |

HEINOUS OFFENCES AND THE 16-18 BAND
For a heinous offence alleged to have been committed by a child who has completed sixteen years, the Juvenile Justice Board makes a preliminary assessment of mental and physical capacity to commit such offence, ability to understand the consequences, and the circumstances. After that assessment the Board may order that the child be tried as an adult before the Children's Court. Do not write that a 16-18-year-old is automatically an adult. Preliminary assessment is mandatory. Children under sixteen are not transferred.

OTHER HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Apprehension is not treated as arrest in the ordinary criminal sense. The child goes to an observation home, not a lock-up, pending production before the Board.
- Adoption of children in need of care and protection is regulated under this Act.
- Institutional and non-institutional care (foster care, sponsorship, after-care) sit with the Child Welfare Committee for the child in need of care and protection.

> **EXAM TIP:** Open with 2015 as current (1986 and 2000 repealed). Draw the two-stream table (Juvenile Justice Board versus Child Welfare Committee). For 16-18 heinous offences write preliminary assessment, never automatic adult trial.

[SN]Child labour Act[/SN]

CHILD AND ADOLESCENT LABOUR (PROHIBITION AND REGULATION) ACT, 1986 (AMENDMENT 2016)
The Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986, as amended in 2016, is the current labour statute for persons under eighteen.

- Complete prohibition of employment of children (under fourteen years) in any occupation or process, with two narrow exceptions: work in a family enterprise after school hours and during vacations, and work as an artist in audio-visual entertainment or sports, both with conditions that the work not affect school education and not be hazardous.
- Adolescents (fourteen to eighteen years) may work, but not in hazardous occupations and processes as listed.
- The Amendment, 2016 aligned the floor age with the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 (free education, 6-14 years).
- Occupier and employer are liable. A rescue is followed by rehabilitation through the statutory fund and the education system, not only by a fine.

INDIA HOOK
A Medical Officer certifying age, fitness, or injury in a workshop is inside this Act. Under fourteen is not a “light work” question except the two statutory exceptions.

> **EXAM TIP:** Under 14: ban in any occupation (family enterprise after school, and entertainment, only with conditions). 14-18: ban in hazardous occupations and processes. Name the 2016 Amendment.

[SN]Prohibition of Child Marriage Act[/SN]

PROHIBITION OF CHILD MARRIAGE ACT, 2006
The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 sets the minimum age of marriage at twenty-one years for a male and eighteen years for a female. A later proposal to raise the age for females to twenty-one is not current law. Do not write it as enacted.

- A child marriage is voidable at the option of the person who was a child at the time of marriage, by a petition filed within the prescribed period after attaining majority.
- Certain marriages are void in the circumstances named in the Act (for example, where the child is taken away from a lawful guardian by enticement, force, or sale, or is married after being trafficked, as provided).
- Whoever performs, conducts, directs, or abets a child marriage commits an offence. The male adult above eighteen who contracts a child marriage, and the parent or guardian who promotes it, are punishable.
- A Child Marriage Prohibition Officer has power to prevent solemnisation and to collect evidence.

Public-health link: early marriage drives adolescent pregnancy, anaemia, school drop-out, and intergenerational undernutrition. Pair this Act with the Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) Rule 3B category for minors and with the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 when the question is “social legislation for the girl child”.

> **EXAM TIP:** Ages 21 (male) and 18 (female) are current. Marriage is voidable, not automatically void in every case. The offence sits on the person who performs or conducts the marriage, the adult male contracting party, and the promoting guardian.

[SN]IMS Act[/SN]

INFANT MILK SUBSTITUTES, FEEDING BOTTLES AND INFANT FOODS (REGULATION OF PRODUCTION, SUPPLY AND DISTRIBUTION) ACT, 1992
The Infant Milk Substitutes, Feeding Bottles and Infant Foods (Regulation of Production, Supply and Distribution) Act, 1992 (IMS Act), amended in 2003, is India’s statute to protect breastfeeding. It implements, in domestic law, the spirit of the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes.

- No person shall advertise infant milk substitutes, feeding bottles, or infant foods, or take part in any such advertisement.
- Containers and labels shall not have pictures of infants or other pictures or text that idealise the use of infant milk substitutes. Required warnings and the superiority of breastfeeding must appear as prescribed.
- No person shall offer inducement to any person for the purpose of promoting the use or sale of infant milk substitutes or feeding bottles or infant foods.
- Health workers shall not accept gifts, contributions, or sponsorship from manufacturers or distributors of these products for promoting them.
- Educational material on infant feeding must include the prescribed points on breastfeeding, the difficulty of reversing a decision not to breastfeed, and the proper use of substitutes only when needed.
- The 2003 Amendment tightened the net around infant foods and promotion through the health system.

INDIA HOOK
This Act is the legal arm of Infant and Young Child Feeding. A resident who only writes “promote breastfeeding” without naming the advertisement ban, the no-infant-picture label rule, and the health-worker gift ban has not answered the statute.

> **EXAM TIP:** 1992, Amendment 2003. Four prohibitions: advertisement; infant pictures on labels; inducements; gifts to health workers. Close with “supports exclusive breastfeeding”.

[SN]Right to Education Act[/SN]

RIGHT OF CHILDREN TO FREE AND COMPULSORY EDUCATION ACT, 2009
The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 (RTE Act) gives effect to Article 21A. Every child of the age of six to fourteen years has a right to free and compulsory elementary education in a neighbourhood school.

- The appropriate Government and the local authority shall establish schools and ensure admission, attendance, and completion of elementary education.
- No child shall be held back, expelled, or required to pass a board examination until completion of elementary education, as provided in the Act.
- Private unaided schools shall admit, to the extent of at least twenty-five per cent of the strength of Class I (or the pre-primary entry class), children belonging to weaker sections and disadvantaged groups in the neighbourhood, and provide free and compulsory elementary education to them. The State reimburses as provided.
- Teachers shall have the prescribed professional qualification. Screening tests for admission and capitation fees are prohibited.
- A School Management Committee, with a majority of parents or guardians, monitors utilisation and the school development plan.

Public-health link: years of schooling, especially of the mother, move infant mortality, immunisation coverage, and fertility. RTE is therefore a health-determinants statute, not only an education statute.

> **EXAM TIP:** Ages 6-14, free and compulsory, neighbourhood school, and 25% seats in private unaided schools for weaker sections and disadvantaged groups. That is the short note.
