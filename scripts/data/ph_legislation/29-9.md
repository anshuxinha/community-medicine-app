OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This section covers who may practise, which clinic may open, and how a paying patient sues. The current statute is the National Medical Commission (NMC) Act, 2019, in place of the repealed Indian Medical Council Act, 1956; the Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 as a State-adoption law, not a nationwide code; and the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (CPA) with Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha (1995). Short notes then cover pharmacy, nursing, dental, and allied professions.

DEFINITIONS
NATIONAL MEDICAL COMMISSION: Statutory body under the 2019 Act that regulates medical education, the national medical register, and ethics, in place of the former Medical Council of India.

CLINICAL ESTABLISHMENT: A hospital, nursing home, clinic, laboratory, imaging unit, or other institution offering diagnosis, treatment, or care under a recognised system of medicine. Armed Forces establishments are excluded from the 2010 Act.

INDIAN MEDICAL REGISTER: National electronic register of licensed medical practitioners maintained by the Ethics and Medical Registration Board from State Medical Registers.

SERVICE (CPA): After Shantha, paid medical attendance is a service. A doctor is not excluded merely because the defendant is a professional.

[LAQ]National Medical Commission: autonomous boards and functions[/LAQ]

NATIONAL MEDICAL COMMISSION ACT, 2019
The NMC Act, 2019 replaced the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956. Object: quality and affordable medical education, an adequate professional workforce, a community-health perspective, a national medical register, and high ethical standards.

Four Autonomous Boards:
1. Under-Graduate Medical Education Board: undergraduate standards and curriculum.
2. Post-Graduate Medical Education Board: postgraduate and super-specialty standards.
3. Medical Assessment and Rating Board: permission, assessment, and rating of institutions.
4. Ethics and Medical Registration Board: conduct, ethics, and the Indian Medical Register.

**Mnemonic:** UP-ME (Under-Graduate, Post-Graduate, Medical Assessment, Ethics)

The Act provides for a National Exit Test as a common final-year undergraduate examination, and as the basis for licensing to practise and for postgraduate admission, as laid down in the statute. Do not write a commencement year unless a Gazette notification for that sitting is before you. It also provides for limited-licence Community Health Providers: persons connected with the modern scientific medical profession who have prescribed qualifications may be granted a limited licence to practise at mid-level as specified. The clause is controversial. Write it as statute, not as a headcount.

No person shall practise modern medicine unless enrolled in the State Medical Register and reflected in the Indian Medical Register, subject to the Act. Foreign qualifications require recognition as prescribed. State Medical Councils continue first-instance registration and many conduct matters.

> **EXAM TIP:** Open with 2019 replacing 1956. Draw four boards with UP-ME. Then Indian Medical Register, National Exit Test as provided in the Act (no invented start year), and limited-licence Community Health Providers as a controversial statutory clause.

[SN]Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010[/SN]

[LAQ]Clinical Establishments Act, 2010[/LAQ]

CLINICAL ESTABLISHMENTS (REGISTRATION AND REGULATION) ACT, 2010
Object: registration and regulation of clinical establishments, with prescribed minimum standards. Public health is a State List subject. This Central Act applies only in the first adopting States, in States that later adopt it under Article 252, and in the Union Territories to which it has been extended. It is not in force in every State.

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) portal clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in (page last update 05-08-2026) states that the Act has been adopted in 19 States and Union Territories, namely Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Mizoram, Rajasthan, Sikkim, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and all Union Territories except the National Capital Territory of Delhi. Several large States run their own establishment statutes instead.

A National Council for Clinical Establishments advises on minimum standards and classification. Adopting States constitute a State Council and a district registering authority. Registration is provisional on application, then permanent after notified minimum standards are met. The certificate and the rates of procedures and services must be displayed.

Section 12(2): every registered clinical establishment has a duty, within the staff and facilities available, to provide such medical examination and treatment as may be required to stabilise the emergency medical condition of any individual who comes or is brought to that establishment.

A Charter of Patients' Rights, drafted by the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) and adopted in National Council guidance, is hosted on the MoHFW portal. It is guidance, not a separate Act.

> **EXAM TIP:** Not nationwide force. Name the 19-State/UT portal list (except NCT of Delhi). Then provisional then permanent registration, display of rates, section 12(2) duty to stabilise, National Council, and the NHRC charter as guidance.

[SN]Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and medical services[/SN]

CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT, 2019
The CPA, 2019 replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986. Three-tier commissions: District, State, and National. Pecuniary limits are as notified (the 2019 scheme uses consideration paid, not compensation claimed, and changed the rupee slabs relative to 1986; do not invent crore figures). Unfair contracts can be struck down. Product liability is a distinct chapter covering the manufacturer, seller, or product-service provider.

Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha (1995) held that medical services fall within “service” unless care is free of charge to every patient. That line is carried into the 2019 scheme. Doctors are not excluded merely because they are professionals. A paying patient may allege deficiency of service, including medical negligence. Purely free government care offered to all remains the classic exclusion.

> **EXAM TIP:** 2019 replacing 1986; District-State-National commissions; pecuniary limits as notified; product liability; unfair contracts. Close with Shantha: paid medical care is a service; professionals are not exempt by title alone.

[SN]Pharmacy, nursing, dental, and allied professional statutes[/SN]

OTHER PROFESSIONAL STATUTES
Pharmacy Act, 1948 remains the parent statute for pharmacist registration. The Pharmacy Council of India frames education regulations and the register of pharmacists. Do not write a National Pharmacy Commission as enacted law. The National Nursing and Midwifery Commission Act, 2023 replaced the Indian Nursing Council Act, 1947. The National Dental Commission Act, 2023 replaced the Dentists Act, 1948.

The National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions Act, 2021 provides for State Councils and a Central Register and State Registers. The Schedule recognises ten categories: Medical Laboratory and Life Sciences; Trauma, Burn Care and Surgical/Anaesthesia related technology; Physiotherapy Professional; Nutrition Science Professional; Ophthalmic Sciences Professional; Occupational Therapy Professional; Community Care, Behavioural Health Sciences and other Professionals; Medical Radiology, Imaging and Therapeutic Technology Professional; Medical Technologists and Physician Associate; Health Information Management and Health Informatic Professional.

> **EXAM TIP:** Pharmacy 1948 is still the parent Act. Nursing 2023 replaced 1947. Dental 2023 replaced 1948. Allied 2021: ten Schedule categories, State Councils, Central and State registers.
