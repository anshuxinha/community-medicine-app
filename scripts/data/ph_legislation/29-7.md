OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This section is the occupational-health statute page in the chapter. Classic short notes still name the Factories Act, 1948 and the Employees’ State Insurance Act, 1948. The current umbrella is the four Labour Codes brought into force from 21 November 2025. During transition, relevant provisions of the older Acts and their rules remain in force where corresponding Code rules are not yet operational. Central Rules under the Codes were notified in May 2026.

Hygiene, pneumoconiosis, and factory inspection technique stay in the Occupational Health chapter. Cover definitions, coverage, hours, benefits, and liability.

DEFINITIONS
FACTORY (Factories Act, 1948): Premises where 10 or more workers are working, or were working on any day of the preceding 12 months, with the aid of power, or 20 or more workers without power, and in any part of which a manufacturing process is being carried on or is ordinarily carried on.

OCCUPIER: The person who has ultimate control over the affairs of the factory. The occupier is liable for compliance with health, safety and welfare provisions.

INSURED PERSON (IP): An employee covered under the Employees’ State Insurance Act, 1948 (or, now, the corresponding social-security provisions of the Code on Social Security, 2020).

LABOUR CODE: A consolidating statute that repeals and replaces a group of older labour Acts. India notified four such Codes with effect from 21 November 2025.

[LAQ]Occupational health legislation[/LAQ]

CURRENT UMBRELLA: FOUR LABOUR CODES
If the question is “occupational health legislation” or “labour laws for health of workers”, open here, then map the older Acts. If the question names the Factories Act or the Employees’ State Insurance Act, skip this opening and write those provisions first.

The four Codes, effective 21 November 2025, rationalise 29 labour laws:
1. Code on Wages, 2019.
2. Industrial Relations Code, 2020.
3. Code on Social Security, 2020.
4. Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020.

**Mnemonic:** WISS
- W: Wages, 2019
- I: Industrial Relations, 2020
- S: Social Security, 2020
- S: Safety (Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions), 2020

High-yield Code points (Press Information Bureau, 21 November 2025)
- Mandatory appointment letters.
- Social security for gig and platform workers. Aggregators contribute 1 to 2 percent of annual turnover, capped at 5 percent of the amount payable to gig and platform workers.
- Free annual health check-up for workers above 40 years.
- Women permitted to work at night, with their consent and with safety measures.
- Employees’ State Insurance Corporation (ESIC) coverage is pan-India; voluntary for establishments with fewer than 10 employees; mandatory even for one employee in hazardous processes.
- Single registration, single licence, and single return.
- Inspector-cum-facilitator (the inspection function is paired with facilitation).
- National Occupational Safety and Health Board.
- Higher factory applicability thresholds than the 10-with-power / 20-without-power rule of 1948.
- Gratuity for fixed-term employees after one year.

Transition: relevant provisions of existing Acts and rules remain in force until the corresponding Code rules operate. Central Rules were notified in May 2026.

HOW TO WRITE THE LONG ANSWER
1. One sentence: occupational health law sets enforceable duties of the occupier and a social-security net for the worker.
2. Four Codes from 21 November 2025 (WISS), plus two or three Code points (appointment letter, gig-worker levy, ESIC expansion, health check-up above 40).
3. Map Factories Act, 1948 and Employees’ State Insurance Act, 1948 as the classic statutes still asked by name.
4. One line each on Mines, Compensation, Plantations, Minimum Wages, Contract Labour.
5. Close: law without inspection (now inspector-cum-facilitator) does not protect the worker.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not invent a new factory threshold number. Write “higher than the 10/20 rule of 1948.”
- Do not declare the 1948 Acts unexaminable. Named-Act questions still want 1948 provisions.
- Do not ignore transition. Older rules remain in force where Code rules are not yet operational.

[SN]Factories Act, 1948[/SN]

FACTORIES ACT, 1948
Object: to regulate labour in factories, and in particular health, safety, welfare, hours of work, and employment of young persons.

Coverage
- Factory: 10 or more workers with power; 20 or more without power.
- The 1976 amendment includes contract labour in the definition of “worker”.
- The 1987 amendment, after the Bhopal disaster, inserted Chapter IVA on hazardous processes, and strengthened occupier liability.

Three chapters the examiner expects by name
- Health: cleanliness, disposal of wastes and effluents, ventilation and temperature, dust and fume, overcrowding, lighting, drinking water, latrines and urinals, spittoons. Cubic space is historically taught as 500 cubic feet per worker.
- Safety: fencing of machinery, work on or near machinery in motion, employment of young persons on dangerous machines, hoists and lifts, excessive weights, protection of eyes, precautions against dangerous fumes, explosive or inflammable dust and gas, fire.
- Welfare: washing facilities, storing and drying of clothing, sitting, first-aid appliances, canteens, shelters, rest rooms and lunch rooms, creches, welfare officers.

Hours and leave (classic teaching)
- Hours of work and a weekly holiday as prescribed in the Act.
- Overtime is paid at twice the ordinary rate of wages.

Notifiable occupational diseases: as listed in the Act and its schedule (classically taught as 29 under the Factories Act; 3 under the Mines Act; 8 under the dock-workers provisions). Examples on the factory schedule include lead poisoning, silicosis, asbestosis, byssinosis and chrome ulceration.

Certifying surgeons: appointed to examine young persons and persons employed in hazardous processes, and to investigate notifiable diseases.

Occupier liability: the occupier has ultimate control and is responsible for health, safety and welfare. After 1987 this includes specific duties for hazardous processes (disclosure, medical examination, health records, site appraisal).

Public-health purpose: primary prevention of occupational disease and injury inside the factory fence.

> **EXAM TIP:** Start with the 10/20 definition, then 1976 (contract labour as worker) and 1987 (Chapter IVA after Bhopal). Write Health, Safety, Welfare as three headings. Add 500 cubic feet, overtime at double wages, certifying surgeons, occupier liability, and “notifiable diseases as scheduled (classically 29).”

[SN]Employees’ State Insurance Act, 1948[/SN]

EMPLOYEES’ STATE INSURANCE ACT, 1948
Object: to provide for certain benefits to employees in case of sickness, maternity and employment injury, and to make provision for related matters.

Machinery: autonomous Employees’ State Insurance Corporation. The Union Labour Minister is the Chairperson.

Coverage
- Establishments with 10 or more employees, as notified.
- Extended to shops, hotels, cinemas, road transport, newspapers, and private medical and educational institutions (the employee threshold is 20 or more in some States for some of these extensions).
- Wage ceiling for coverage: Rs 21,000 per month from 01.01.2017 (Rs 25,000 per month for persons with disability).
- Contribution commonly taught from July 2019: employer 3.25 percent and employee 0.75 percent of wages (total 4 percent).

Six cash and medical benefits
1. Medical benefit to the Insured Person and family.
2. Sickness benefit: about 70 percent of wages, for a period as prescribed (classically up to 91 days in two consecutive benefit periods).
3. Maternity benefit: 26 weeks at about full wages; 6 weeks for miscarriage.
4. Disablement benefit: temporary disablement about 90 percent of wages; permanent disablement according to loss of earning capacity.
5. Dependants’ benefit: periodical payment to dependants of an Insured Person who dies of an employment injury.
6. Funeral expenses: not exceeding Rs 15,000.

Unemployment allowance exists as a scheme under the Corporation, notably the Atal Beemit Vyakti Kalyan Yojana. Name the scheme. Do not invent a rupee figure.

Where the Employees’ State Insurance Act applies, the employer is exempt from the corresponding liability under the Employees’ Compensation Act, 1923 (formerly the Workmen’s Compensation Act).

Public-health purpose: social insurance that buys medical care and wage replacement when a covered worker is sick, pregnant, disabled, or dead from employment injury.

**Mnemonic:** MSM-DDF
- M: Medical (IP and family)
- S: Sickness (about 70 percent of wages)
- M: Maternity (26 weeks; miscarriage 6 weeks)
- D: Disablement (temporary about 90 percent; permanent by earning-capacity loss)
- D: Dependants’ benefit
- F: Funeral expenses, not exceeding Rs 15,000

> **EXAM TIP:** Write Corporation plus Union Labour Minister as Chairperson, then Rs 21,000 (Rs 25,000 if disabled) from 01.01.2017, then 3.25 plus 0.75 from July 2019. Enumerate MSM-DDF. Add Atal Beemit Vyakti Kalyan Yojana by name and the exemption from the Employees’ Compensation Act.

OTHER LABOUR STATUTES STILL ASKED (ONE PARAGRAPH EACH)
MINES ACT, 1952. Regulates health, safety and welfare of workers employed in mines: hours of work, medical examination, certifying surgeons, and notification of occupational diseases (classically taught as three notifiable diseases under this Act). Functions now sit under the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020.

EMPLOYEES’ COMPENSATION ACT, 1923 (formerly the Workmen’s Compensation Act). Imposes on the employer a liability to pay compensation for personal injury caused by accident arising out of and in the course of employment, and for specified occupational diseases. It applies where the Employees’ State Insurance Act does not. Functions now sit under the Code on Social Security, 2020.

PLANTATIONS LABOUR ACT, 1951. Provides for health, welfare, hours of work, housing and medical facilities for workers in tea, coffee, rubber, cinchona and other notified plantations. Now mapped to the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020.

MINIMUM WAGES ACT, 1948. Empowers appropriate governments to fix and revise minimum rates of wages in scheduled employments. The public-health link is nutrition and the wage floor. Now mapped to the Code on Wages, 2019.

CONTRACT LABOUR (REGULATION AND ABOLITION) ACT, 1970. Regulates employment of contract labour, requires registration of principal employers and licensing of contractors, and permits abolition of contract labour in certain circumstances. The 1976 reading of “worker” under the Factories Act already pulls contract labour into factory protection. Now mapped to the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020.
