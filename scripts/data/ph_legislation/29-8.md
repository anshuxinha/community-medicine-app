OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This leaf is the legal home for environment, pollution, and waste statutes that a community-medicine answer must name. Operational colour-coding drills, spill kits, and Common Bio-Medical Waste Treatment Facility (CBMWTF) engineering stay in the Hospital Waste Management chapter. Write this leaf as who makes the standard, who gives consent, who is the occupier, and what the penalty hook is.

Bhopal is the historical peg for two statutes on this page: the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (the umbrella) and the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 (immediate relief on no-fault principles).

DEFINITIONS
ENVIRONMENT (Environment (Protection) Act, 1986): Water, air and land, and the inter-relationship which exists among and between water, air and land, and human beings, other living creatures, plants, micro-organisms and property.

ENVIRONMENTAL POLLUTANT: Any solid, liquid or gaseous substance present in such concentration as may be, or tend to be, injurious to the environment.

BIO-MEDICAL WASTE: Waste generated during diagnosis, treatment or immunisation of human beings or animals, or in research, or in the production or testing of biologicals, as defined in the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016.

OCCUPIER (Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016): The person having administrative control over the institution that generates bio-medical waste. Distinct from the operator, who owns or controls a CBMWTF.

OPERATOR: The person who owns, or controls, or operates a Common Bio-Medical Waste Treatment Facility.

[LAQ]Environmental health legislation[/LAQ]

[SN]Environment Protection Act[/SN]

ENVIRONMENT (PROTECTION) ACT, 1986
Umbrella statute enacted after Bhopal. It fills gaps between the Water Act and the Air Act, and gives the Central Government a general power to protect and improve the environment.

Powers the examiner expects
- To set standards for emission or discharge of environmental pollutants.
- To restrict the areas in which any industries, operations or processes shall not be carried out, or shall be carried out subject to safeguards.
- To inspect premises, plant, equipment, machinery, manufacturing or other processes, materials or substances, and to take samples.
- To issue directions, including closure, prohibition or regulation of any industry, operation or process, and stoppage of supply of electricity, water or any other service.
- Penalty for contravention of the Act, the rules, orders or directions.

Public-health purpose: one Central handle for chemical, industrial and residual environmental risk that disease-specific or medium-specific Acts do not cover.

WATER (PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF POLLUTION) ACT, 1974
Constitutes the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and the State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs). A new or existing discharge of sewage or trade effluent needs the Board’s consent to establish and consent to operate. The Boards lay down standards and can take legal action for discharge without consent or in breach of conditions.

AIR (PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF POLLUTION) ACT, 1981
Provides for declaration of air pollution control areas. In those areas, operation of an industrial plant requires the consent of the State Board. CPCB and the SPCBs implement the Act and advise on ambient and emission standards.

HOW TO WRITE THE LONG ANSWER
1. One-sentence definition of environmental health legislation (standards, consent, liability).
2. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 as the post-Bhopal umbrella (standards, restrict industry, inspect, penalty).
3. Water Act, 1974 and Air Act, 1981 (Boards; consent to establish or operate; air pollution control areas).
4. Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016 (occupier versus operator; four colours; 48 hours; authorisation; annual report).
5. One line each: Solid Waste 2016; Plastic Waste 2016 with later extended producer responsibility including 2022; E-Waste Rules 2022; Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules 2016.
6. Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991; Insecticides Act, 1962; Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000; Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 as amended 2019.
7. Close: primary prevention through standards and consent, and a no-fault safety net when hazardous substances escape.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not write the Bio-Medical Waste (Management and Handling) Rules, 1998 as if they were still in force. They are superseded by the 2016 Rules.
- Do not dump incinerator temperatures here; that is the Hospital Waste chapter.
- Do not invent a 2024 year for plastic-waste rules. Write 2016 as the base, with later amendments including 2022 extended producer responsibility.

OTHER ENVIRONMENT AND WASTE INSTRUMENTS (SHORT NOTE DEPTH)
SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT RULES, 2016. Duties of waste generators (source segregation into wet, dry and domestic hazardous fractions), duties of local bodies for collection and processing, and restrictions on dumping of mixed waste. The public-health purpose is to cut vector breeding, leachate, and burning of mixed municipal waste.

PLASTIC WASTE MANAGEMENT RULES, 2016. Base rules for plastic-waste segregation, collection and processing. Later amendments, including 2022, built out extended producer responsibility: producers, importers and brand owners must collect back and process plastic packaging they place on the market.

E-WASTE (MANAGEMENT) RULES, 2022. Regulate electrical and electronic equipment waste through extended producer responsibility, authorised dismantlers and recyclers, and restrictions on informal dumping and burning of circuit boards.

HAZARDOUS AND OTHER WASTES (MANAGEMENT AND TRANSBOUNDARY MOVEMENT) RULES, 2016. Authorisation for generating, storing, treating and disposing of hazardous waste, and control of transboundary movement, consistent with the Basel Convention approach.

PUBLIC LIABILITY INSURANCE ACT, 1991. Imposes no-fault (absolute) liability on the owner handling a hazardous substance. The owner must take out insurance so that victims of an accident receive immediate relief, without first proving negligence. This is the statutory Bhopal lesson on compensation speed.

INSECTICIDES ACT, 1962. Regulates import, manufacture, sale, transport, distribution and use of insecticides with a view to prevent risk to human beings or animals, and for allied matters. Licensing and a Central Insecticides Board are the usual examination hooks.

NOISE POLLUTION (REGULATION AND CONTROL) RULES, 2000. Ambient noise standards by area (industrial, commercial, residential, silence zone) and restrictions on loudspeakers and public-address systems, especially at night and around hospitals, educational institutions and courts.

MOTOR VEHICLES ACT, 1988, AS AMENDED 2019. Injury-prevention provisions the examiner wants: drink-driving, mandatory helmet, seat-belt, and hit-and-run compensation. One public-health line more: emission norms under motor-vehicle law reduce particulate and other exhaust exposures that contribute to non-communicable disease, including lung cancer.

> **EXAM TIP:** Open with the 1986 Act as the Bhopal umbrella. Then Water 1974 and Air 1981 with CPCB, SPCB and consent. Then the 2016 bio-medical waste Rules as the hospital statute. Close with Public Liability Insurance, 1991 and one line on the Motor Vehicles Act, 2019 amendment (helmet, seat-belt, drink-driving, hit-and-run).

[SN]Biomedical waste management rules[/SN]

BIO-MEDICAL WASTE MANAGEMENT RULES, 2016
These Rules supersede the Bio-Medical Waste (Management and Handling) Rules, 1998. They are notified under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. Central Pollution Control Board guidelines fill in the operational detail.

Persons
- Occupier: the person with administrative control over the health-care institution that generates the waste. The occupier must segregate at source, pre-treat laboratory waste as required, hand waste to an authorised facility, and not store it beyond the permitted time.
- Operator: the person who owns or operates the CBMWTF. The operator collects, transports, treats and disposes of waste from member occupiers.

Duties the short note must name
- Authorisation from the prescribed authority (the State Pollution Control Board or Pollution Control Committee).
- Segregation at the point of generation into the four colour streams below.
- Treatment and disposal within 48 hours.
- Annual report to the prescribed authority.
- Occupier shall not establish an on-site treatment plant if a CBMWTF is available within the prescribed distance, except as allowed by the Rules.

Colour code (legal SN table; technique stays in the Hospital Waste Management chapter)

| Colour | Container | Waste (examination list) | Legal fate in one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| YELLOW | Non-chlorinated bag or container | Human and animal anatomical waste; soiled waste; expired or discarded medicines; chemical waste; discarded linen; microbiology and biotechnology laboratory waste after pre-treatment | Incineration or plasma pyrolysis (deep burial only where no CBMWTF, as permitted) |
| RED | Non-chlorinated bag | Recyclable contaminated plastics: tubing, bottles, intravenous sets, catheters, gloves | Autoclave (or equivalent), then recycle |
| WHITE | Translucent puncture-proof, leak-proof, tamper-proof container | Sharps: needles, syringes with needles, scalpels | Sterilise, then mutilate or send to an authorised recycler or sharp pit |
| BLUE | Puncture-proof box or container | Glassware; metallic body implants | Disinfect or autoclave, then recycle |

Public-health purpose: stop hospital-acquired infection, needle-stick injury, and environmental release of untreated anatomical, chemical and sharp waste.

**Mnemonic:** YRWB
- Y: Yellow (anatomical, soiled, medicines, chemical, linen, laboratory after pre-treatment)
- R: Red (recyclable contaminated plastics; autoclave then recycle)
- W: White translucent puncture-proof (sharps)
- B: Blue (glassware and metallic implants)

> **EXAM TIP:** Say “2016 Rules, not 1998.” Define occupier versus operator and name the CBMWTF. Draw the four-colour table (YRWB). Write treatment within 48 hours, authorisation, and annual report. Point to CPCB guidelines and send incinerator temperatures to the Hospital Waste chapter.
