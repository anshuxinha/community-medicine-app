OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This leaf is the legal home for statutes that keep food safe, keep medicines genuine, restrict tobacco, and control narcotics. Programme operations stay elsewhere: nutrition programmes in the nutrition chapter, the National Tobacco Control Programme (NTCP) under non-communicable diseases. The Mid-Day Meal scheme, now Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman (PM POSHAN), is a feeding programme. It is not a provision of the National Food Security Act, 2013.

A resident who writes only the year of an Act will lose the short note. The expected answer is object, machinery, two or three operative provisions, and the public-health purpose.

DEFINITIONS
FOOD SAFETY AND STANDARDS ACT, 2006: The current Central statute that consolidates food law, establishes the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI), and regulates manufacture, storage, distribution, sale and import of food. It repealed the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954. The Act was brought into force on 5 August 2011.

FOOD BUSINESS OPERATOR (FBO): A person by whom the food business is carried on or owned. The FBO must obtain a licence or a registration, as prescribed, before operating.

UNSAFE FOOD: Food whose nature, substance or quality is so affected as to render it injurious to health. Distinct from sub-standard or misbranded food, which attract graded (usually lighter) penalties.

DRUGS AND COSMETICS ACT, 1940: The statute that regulates import, manufacture, distribution and sale of drugs and cosmetics in India, through Central and State licensing.

CIGARETTES AND OTHER TOBACCO PRODUCTS (PROHIBITION OF ADVERTISEMENT AND REGULATION OF TRADE AND COMMERCE, PRODUCTION, SUPPLY AND DISTRIBUTION) ACT, 2003: The comprehensive tobacco-control statute, abbreviated COTPA. Assented to on 18 May 2003 and notified on 25 February 2004.

NARCOTIC DRUGS AND PSYCHOTROPIC SUBSTANCES ACT, 1985: The statute that prohibits, controls and regulates narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances, while permitting medical and scientific use under licence.

FOOD SAFETY AND STANDARDS ACT, 2006
Do not open a current answer with the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954. That Act is repealed. The living statute is the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, in force from 5 August 2011.

Object: to consolidate the laws relating to food and to establish FSSAI for laying down science-based standards and for regulating food business, so as to ensure availability of safe and wholesome food for human consumption.

Machinery
- FSSAI is the statutory Central authority. It frames standards and regulations and oversees enforcement with the States.
- Scientific Committee and Scientific Panels advise the Authority on risk assessment and on standards.
- Every FBO must be registered or licensed. Registration is the lighter obligation for small operators; a licence is required for larger food businesses, as prescribed.
- Food Safety Officer: inspects premises, draws samples, seizes unsafe food, and investigates.
- Designated Officer: the licensing authority for the area; grants, suspends or cancels licences and decides whether a case should go for adjudication or prosecution.
- Adjudicating Officer: imposes the graded monetary penalties created by the Act.
- Food recall: an FBO who has placed unsafe food on the market must recall it and inform the authorities.

Graded penalties are the examination contrast with 1954. Many contraventions are dealt with by administrative penalty rather than by a criminal trial. Unsafe food is treated more severely than sub-standard or misbranded food.

Public-health purpose: primary prevention of food-borne disease, chemical adulteration, and micronutrient harm from an unsafe food supply.

**Mnemonic:** F-B-O-P-R-S
- F: FSSAI
- B: Business operator licence or registration
- O: Officers (Food Safety, Designated, Adjudicating)
- P: Graded penalties
- R: Recall
- S: Scientific Committee and Scientific Panels

NATIONAL FOOD SECURITY ACT, 2013
This Act converts subsidised foodgrain from a welfare scheme into a legal entitlement.

Coverage, as enacted: up to 75 percent of the rural population and up to 50 percent of the urban population. Eligible households are priority households and Antyodaya households (the poorest, under the Antyodaya Anna Yojana). They receive foodgrain at subsidised prices through the Targeted Public Distribution System.

Maternity benefit: every pregnant woman and lactating mother is entitled to a maternity benefit of not less than Rs 6000, as provided in the Act, except those already covered in regular employment with the same or a better benefit. The cash maternity entitlement is now delivered largely through the Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY).

What this Act is not: PM POSHAN (the former Mid-Day Meal) is a separate feeding programme. Do not write it as a clause of the 2013 Act.

Public-health purpose: statutory access to staple foodgrain and a maternity cash entitlement, aimed at hunger, undernutrition, and maternal nutrition.

HOW TO WRITE THE NUTRITION-AND-FOOD-SAFETY SHORT NOTE
1. One line: the 1954 Act is repealed; the 2006 Act is in force from 5 August 2011.
2. FSSAI, FBO licence or registration, the three officers, graded penalties, recall, Scientific Committee and Panels.
3. One paragraph on the National Food Security Act, 2013 (coverage 75 percent rural and 50 percent urban; priority and Antyodaya; maternity benefit not less than Rs 6000 via PMMVY).
4. Close: food law prevents adulteration and unsafe food; food-security law creates a justiciable claim to grain.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not treat the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954 as current law.
- Do not dump FSSAI logo campaigns as if they were sections of the Act.
- Do not write PM POSHAN as a provision of the National Food Security Act, 2013.

> **EXAM TIP:** Open with repeal of the 1954 Act and the 5 August 2011 commencement. Name FSSAI, the Food Business Operator, the three officers, graded penalties, and recall. Add the 2013 Act with 75/50 coverage and maternity benefit not less than Rs 6000 (PMMVY as the delivery scheme). Close on primary prevention of food-borne disease plus a legal right to grain.

DRUGS AND COSMETICS ACT, 1940
Object: to regulate import, manufacture, distribution and sale of drugs and cosmetics, so that what reaches the patient is of standard quality.

Licensing
- Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO), under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, is the Central licensing and regulatory authority (new drugs, specified biologicals, imported drugs, and other Central subjects as notified).
- State licensing authorities license manufacture and sale of most other drugs and cosmetics in the State.
- A drug that is misbranded, adulterated or spurious is prohibited.

Examination meanings (use these words)
- Misbranded: the label is false or misleading, or the drug is an imitation in presentation, or it is not labelled as required.
- Adulterated: filthy, putrid or decomposed; mixed with a substance that reduces quality; or not of the claimed standard of strength, quality or purity.
- Spurious: a substitute for another drug; manufactured by an unauthorised person; or bearing the name of another manufacturer.

High-yield Schedules under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules
- Schedule H: drugs to be sold only on the prescription of a Registered Medical Practitioner.
- Schedule H1: a tighter prescription schedule (many antimicrobials sit here) with additional record of supply.
- Schedule X: narcotic and specified psychotropic drugs; special licence and records.

Public-health purpose: keeps the therapeutic chain free of fake, sub-standard and unrestricted prescription medicines.

DRUGS AND MAGIC REMEDIES (OBJECTIONABLE ADVERTISEMENTS) ACT, 1954
The year of the Act is 1954. It prohibits advertisement of magic remedies and of drugs for specified conditions listed in the Schedule, including improvement of sexual function and correction of menstrual disorders. A magic remedy includes a talisman, mantra, kavacha, or any alleged miraculous substance offered as a cure. The public-health purpose is to stop exploitation of patients and delay of rational treatment.

> **EXAM TIP:** Write Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation plus State licensing, then misbranded, adulterated and spurious in one line each, then Schedules H, H1 and X. Add the 1954 magic-remedies Act in two sentences (year 1954; scheduled conditions including sexual function and menstrual disorders).

CIGARETTES AND OTHER TOBACCO PRODUCTS ACT, 2003
Write the short title as COTPA, 2003, then the four pillars. Assented 18 May 2003; notified 25 February 2004. This leaf is the Act. NTCP is the operational programme that implements tobacco control; do not substitute the programme for the statute.

India is a Party to the World Health Organization Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (WHO FCTC). COTPA is the principal domestic statute that gives effect to that Convention.

Four pillars
1. Smoke-free public places. The smoke-free rules came into force on 2 October 2008. Specified public places must display the prescribed no-smoking signage.
2. Ban on advertisement, promotion and sponsorship of cigarettes and other tobacco products.
3. Prohibition of sale to and by persons below 18 years, and prohibition of sale within 100 yards of educational institutions.
4. Specified health warnings, including pictorial warnings, on packs.

Pictorial warnings, as already taught with NTCP: from 1 April 2016, warnings occupy 85 percent of the principal display area on both sides of the pack (60 percent picture plus 25 percent text).

Gutka: the nationwide restriction on gutka and similar products has been implemented through Food Safety and Standards Regulations (treating gutka as a food product with tobacco and nicotine), not as a COTPA section.

Public-health purpose: COTPA is the high-yield statute for cancer prevention (and for prevention of other tobacco-related non-communicable disease). A question on “legislation as cancer prevention” should open with COTPA, then one line on pictorial warnings and the 100-yard rule.

**Mnemonic:** SWAP
- S: Smoke-free public places (rules 2 October 2008; signage)
- W: Warnings, including 85 percent pictorial warnings from 1 April 2016
- A: Advertisement, promotion and sponsorship banned
- P: Protection of persons below 18, and the 100-yard rule

> **EXAM TIP:** For cancer-prevention legislation, open with COTPA (18 May 2003 / 25 February 2004) and WHO FCTC. Write the four SWAP pillars. Put 85 percent (60 plus 25) from 1 April 2016 and the 100-yard rule in the first half of the page. One line that NTCP is operations, not the Act. One line that gutka restriction rides on food-safety regulations.

[SN]Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985[/SN]

NARCOTIC DRUGS AND PSYCHOTROPIC SUBSTANCES ACT, 1985
Object: to prohibit, control and regulate operations relating to narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances, and to provide for forfeiture of property derived from illicit traffic.

Scope: cannabis, coca, the opium poppy, and psychotropic substances, together with preparations made from them.

The Act criminalises production, manufacture, possession, sale, transport, import, export and use, except for medical and scientific purposes carried on under licence. Penalties are harsh, especially for trafficking.

The 2014 amendment distinguished the user from the trafficker more clearly, so that the user can be directed toward treatment rather than treated only as a trafficker.

Public-health purpose: suppress illicit supply, while keeping a legal channel for morphine and other essential narcotic drugs used in palliative and medical care.

> **EXAM TIP:** Write prohibition-control-regulation, name cannabis, coca, opium poppy and psychotropic substances, state that medical and scientific use is lawful only under licence, and add one line that the 2014 amendment separated the user (treatment) from the trafficker. Do not invent section numbers or quantities.
