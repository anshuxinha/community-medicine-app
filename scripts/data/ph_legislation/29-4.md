OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
This section is the rights cluster: mental health, disability, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), and transgender persons. These statutes are written as justiciable rights, not as charity. The Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 replaced the Mental Health Act, 1987. The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 replaced the Persons with Disabilities (Equal Opportunities, Protection of Rights and Full Participation) Act, 1995. Examiners still trap candidates who quote 1987 or 1995 as current. The United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD) is the international peg for both the 2016 and 2017 Acts.

DEFINITIONS
MENTAL ILLNESS (MENTAL HEALTHCARE ACT, 2017): A substantial disorder of thinking, mood, perception, orientation, or memory that grossly impairs judgement, behaviour, capacity to recognise reality, or ability to meet the ordinary demands of life. Mental conditions associated with the abuse of alcohol and drugs are included. Mental retardation is not mental illness under this Act.

PERSON WITH BENCHMARK DISABILITY: A person with not less than forty per cent of a specified disability, as certified by the certifying authority.

PERSON WITH DISABILITY: A person with long-term physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory impairment which, in interaction with barriers, hinders full and effective participation in society equally with others.

TRANSGENDER PERSON: A person whose gender does not match the gender assigned at birth. The statutory definition includes trans-man and trans-woman, person with intersex variations, genderqueer, and persons with socio-cultural identities such as kinner, hijra, aravani, and jogta, as named in the Act.

[SN]Mental Healthcare Act[/SN]

[LAQ]Mental Healthcare Act 2017[/LAQ]

MENTAL HEALTHCARE ACT, 2017
The Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 (MHCA) came into force in 2018. It repealed the Mental Health Act, 1987. It is a rights-based statute aligned with the UNCRPD. Object: to provide for mental healthcare and services for persons with mental illness, and to protect, promote, and fulfil their rights.

DEFINITION AND SHIFT FROM 1987
The 1987 Act was a custodial statute (licensing of psychiatric hospitals, reception orders). The 2017 Act starts from rights, capacity, and the least restrictive alternative. Mental retardation is excluded from “mental illness” and is handled under disability law. Capacity is presumed. A person is not deemed to lack capacity merely because of a mental illness diagnosis.

RIGHTS (SECTION 18 AND COMPANIONS)
- Right to access mental health care and treatment from mental health services run or funded by the appropriate Government (section 18). The Government shall make sufficient provision, including at the district level, as far as reasonably possible.
- Right to community living; right to live with dignity; protection from cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment; no chaining.
- Right to equality and non-discrimination, including that mental illness shall be treated on a par with physical illness for insurance (section 21). Insurers shall make provision for medical insurance for treatment of mental illness on the same basis as is available for treatment of physical illness.
- Right to information, confidentiality, access to medical records, and legal aid.
- Right to make an advance directive: a person may specify how they wish to be treated (or not treated) for a mental illness, and who their nominated representative shall be, if they cease to have capacity.
- Nominated representative: appointed by the person, or, if not, in the statutory order of preference (relative, caregiver, then a person appointed by the Mental Health Review Board).
- Least restrictive care: admission and treatment shall use the alternative that least restricts the rights of the person.

DECIMINALISATION OF ATTEMPTED SUICIDE (SECTION 115)
Any person who attempts to commit suicide shall be presumed, unless proved otherwise, to have severe stress, and shall not be tried and punished for the alleged offence of attempt to suicide. The appropriate Government has a duty to provide care, treatment, and rehabilitation to reduce the risk of recurrence. Write this as “shall not be tried for attempt to suicide”. Do not invent a section number of the successor criminal code.

PROHIBITED OR RESTRICTED TREATMENTS
- Sterilisation shall not be performed as a treatment for mental illness.
- Unmodified electro-convulsive therapy is not to be performed; electro-convulsive therapy, where used, follows the restrictions in the Act and Rules (including the bar on unmodified treatment, and restrictions in minors).
- Psychosurgery only with informed consent and the approval of the Mental Health Review Board, as provided.
- No chaining. No tonsuring or compulsory labour as a form of treatment.

AUTHORITIES
- Central Mental Health Authority and State Mental Health Authority: register mental health establishments, maintain registers of professionals, and advise government.
- Mental Health Review Board: reviews supported admissions, advance directives, and nominated representatives, and hears complaints of rights violations.

NATIONAL CONTEXT
India moved from a colonial Lunacy Act (1912) through the 1987 custodial Act to a UNCRPD statute. District Mental Health Programme is the service arm. Section 18 is the line that turns a programme into a justiciable duty. Section 115 is the line that turns a casualty attempt into a patient, not an accused.

CONCLUSION
Open with 2017, in force 2018, repeal of 1987. Define mental illness (and exclude mental retardation). Recite section 18, advance directive, nominated representative, least restrictive care, section 21 insurance parity, section 115, no chaining, no sterilisation as treatment. Close with the two Authorities and the Review Board.

> **EXAM TIP:** Rights, not reception orders. Four section numbers win the paper: 18 (access), 21 (insurance parity), 115 (no trial for attempt to suicide), plus advance directive and nominated representative. State that 1987 is repealed.

[SN]Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act[/SN]

RIGHTS OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES ACT, 2016
The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 (RPwD Act) replaced the Persons with Disabilities Act, 1995. It gives effect to the UNCRPD. Specified disabilities rose from seven to twenty-one. Reservation in government jobs rose from three per cent to four per cent. Benchmark disability is forty per cent.

OPERATIVE PROVISIONS
- Equality, non-discrimination, and reasonable accommodation. Legal capacity is recognised. A person with disability enjoys legal capacity on an equal basis with others, with support where needed.
- Education: inclusive education, and duties of educational institutions (accessible buildings, reasonable accommodation, and non-denial of admission).
- Employment: four per cent reservation in government establishments for persons with benchmark disabilities, with the distribution among specified disability groups as notified. A grievance mechanism and incentives for the private sector are provided.
- Accessibility of buildings, transport, and information and communication technology, in accordance with standards and time-lines notified.
- Health: barrier-free access to hospitals, priority in attendance and treatment, and prevention of occurrence of disabilities as far as possible through the health system.
- Guardianship, where needed, is limited and is ordered by the designated authority after considering support needs.
- Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities at the Centre, and State Commissioners, hear complaints and monitor.

The Schedule specifies twenty-one disabilities (seven under the 1995 Act). Recite the count and two or three examples (locomotor disability, blindness, hearing impairment, intellectual disability, mental illness, autism spectrum disorder, blood disorders, multiple disabilities). Do not invent a twenty-second category.

INDIA HOOK
Certification of benchmark disability (forty per cent) is the Medical Officer's daily contact with this Act. Unique Disability Identity is the programme face. The statute is what makes a ramp, a reserved post, and a refusal of admission justiciable.

> **EXAM TIP:** 2016 replaced 1995. Three figures: 21 disabilities, 4% job reservation (was 3%), benchmark 40%. Then accessibility, inclusive education, legal capacity, Chief Commissioner.

[SN]National Trust Act[/SN]

NATIONAL TRUST ACT, 1999
The National Trust for the Welfare of Persons with Autism, Cerebral Palsy, Mental Retardation and Multiple Disabilities Act, 1999 creates a statutory Trust for those four groups. It is not a general disability Act. That role now sits with the RPwD Act, 2016.

- A Local Level Committee (District Magistrate or the District Collector, a person with disability, and a registered-organisation representative, as provided) can appoint a guardian for a person with any of the four disabilities who needs one, and can remove a guardian who is not acting in the person's interest.
- The Trust supports programmes for independent living, respite, and family support (the named schemes change; do not invent budget figures).

> **EXAM TIP:** Four conditions only: autism, cerebral palsy, mental retardation, multiple disabilities. Name the Local Level Committee and guardianship. Then send the rest of the answer to the 2016 Act.

[SN]HIV AIDS Act[/SN]

HUMAN IMMUNODEFICIENCY VIRUS AND ACQUIRED IMMUNE DEFICIENCY SYNDROME (PREVENTION AND CONTROL) ACT, 2017
The Human Immunodeficiency Virus and Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017 (HIV and AIDS Act) is India's anti-discrimination and treatment-access statute for HIV.

- No person shall discriminate against a protected person in employment, education, health care, housing, insurance, standing for public or private office, or other listed settings.
- Informed consent is required for an HIV test, except as provided (for example, a court order, or certain donation and epidemiological settings as named).
- Confidentiality of HIV-related information is the rule. Disclosure is permitted only in the situations listed (to another health-care provider as necessary for treatment, to a court, or under the partner-notification protocol when the person refuses to inform the partner and there is a significant risk, after counselling, as provided).
- Hate and discriminatory propaganda targeting protected persons is prohibited.
- An Ombudsman in each State inquires into violations of the Act in health-care settings and other notified matters.
- The State has a duty to provide, as far as possible, antiretroviral therapy (ART) and opportunistic-infection management.

**Mnemonic:** EE-HHI
- E: Employment
- E: Education
- H: Health care
- H: Housing
- I: Insurance

INDIA HOOK
This Act is the legal twin of the National AIDS Control Programme. Write consent, confidentiality, Ombudsman, and the duty to provide ART. Do not write that testing can be done “for the husband's sake” without consent.

> **EXAM TIP:** 2017. Recite EE-HHI (no discrimination), informed consent, confidentiality, Ombudsman, prohibition of hate propaganda, and the State's duty to provide ART as far as possible.

[SN]Transgender Persons Act[/SN]

TRANSGENDER PERSONS (PROTECTION OF RIGHTS) ACT, 2019
The Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019 provides for recognition of identity, non-discrimination, and welfare.

- A transgender person may apply to the District Magistrate for a certificate of identity, in the manner prescribed. A revised certificate may be issued after surgery if the person so applies, as provided.
- No person or establishment shall discriminate in education, employment, health care, housing, or access to goods, facilities, and opportunities available to the public.
- The appropriate Government shall take welfare measures, including rescue, rehabilitation, vocational training, and access to health services including sex-reassignment and hormonal therapy counselling as provided in the Rules.
- A National Council for Transgender Persons advises the Central Government and monitors.
- Offences include compelling a transgender person to forced or bonded labour, denying right of public passage, forcing them to leave a household, and physical, sexual, verbal, emotional, or economic abuse, as listed.

Do not invent reservation percentages. The Act as taught does not enact a job-quota figure comparable to the four per cent under the RPwD Act.

> **EXAM TIP:** Identity certificate from the District Magistrate, a flat non-discrimination clause, welfare duties of the State, and the National Council. No quota figure unless a later notified reservation is named in the question.
