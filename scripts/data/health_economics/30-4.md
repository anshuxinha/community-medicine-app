OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Consequences of health programmes include changes in health, resources freed, and other valued effects (convenience, information, reassurance). This section covers the measurement and valuation of health gain: natural units, preference-based generic units, and money. The quality-adjusted life-year (QALY) is the unit of cost-utility analysis. The disability-adjusted life-year (DALY) is a generic health-gap unit used in burden-of-disease work. Willingness to pay (WTP) is the usual route when consequences are put into money for cost-benefit analysis.

DEFINITIONS
QUALITY-ADJUSTED LIFE-YEAR (QALY): A generic measure of health gain that multiplies duration in a health state by a preference weight for that state. Full health has weight 1. Dead has weight 0. States worse than dead can take negative weights in some systems. One QALY is one year of life in full health, or an equivalent combination of time and quality (for example two years at weight 0.5).

In the conventional calculation, the quality-adjustment weight for each health state is multiplied by the time in the state (which may be discounted) and the products are summed. The QALY can capture reduced morbidity (quality) and reduced mortality (quantity) in one number.

DISABILITY-ADJUSTED LIFE-YEAR (DALY): A measure of overall disease burden, expressed as years of healthy life lost. One DALY equals one lost year of healthy life. DALY = years of life lost (YLL) + years lived with disability (YLD). YLL uses deaths multiplied by standard remaining life expectancy. YLD uses incident (or prevalent) cases × duration × a disability weight from 0 (full health) to 1 (dead).

HEALTH-ADJUSTED LIFE EXPECTANCY (HALE): The equivalent number of years a person can expect to live in full health, after adjusting life expectancy for time spent in less-than-full health.

WILLINGNESS TO PAY (WTP): The maximum amount an individual (or, aggregated, a population) would give up to obtain a specified health effect or programme. It is a stated-preference route to a money value when markets do not price the effect.

[SN]Quality Adjusted Life Years (QALY)[/SN]

[LAQ]QALY and cost-utility analysis[/LAQ]

QALY: CONSTRUCTION
Without the intervention, health-related quality of life follows a lower path and death occurs earlier. With the intervention, decline is slower and death is later. The area between the two curves is QALYs gained.

That area can be taught as two parts:
- Area A: quality improvement during years the person would have lived anyway.
- Area B: extra years of life, themselves quality-adjusted.

Preference weights come from:
1. Visual analogue scale (VAS): mark a state on a line from dead to full health. Simple; theoretically weaker as a utility.
2. Time trade-off (TTO): how many years in full health are equivalent to a longer time in the impaired state.
3. Standard gamble (SG): what risk of death (or worse) would be accepted to return to full health. Closest to classical utility theory; harder to administer.

Whose values? Generic instruments used in published studies typically apply a tariff from the general public, not from the patient in the trial. Examiners expect that contrast: patients who live in a state may rate it higher (adaptation) than the public imagining it.

Generic preference-based instruments classify patients into health states with a questionnaire, then apply a tariff. Examples a resident may name: EuroQol five-dimension (EQ-5D), Short Form six-dimension (SF-6D), Health Utilities Index (HUI). Mapping from a disease-specific scale onto a generic instrument is sometimes used when a trial did not collect a preference-based measure.

Criticisms of QALYs (enough for a short paragraph, not a philosophy paper): age and severity weighting debates; whether a QALY is of equal social value to everyone; whose preferences count; whether process of care (how treatment feels) is captured; states worse than dead. Still the most widely used generic denominator for cost-utility analysis.

Cost-utility analysis reports incremental cost per QALY gained. That ratio is interpreted with a threshold under decision rules.

> **EXAM TIP:** Definition (time × weight, 0 to 1) → draw the area between "with" and "without" curves and label A (quality) and B (quantity) → name VAS, TTO, SG in one line each → one criticism → "CUA uses cost per QALY."

[SN]Disability Adjusted Life Years (DALY)[/SN]

DALY
The Global Burden of Disease project uses DALYs so that premature death and non-fatal disability can be added. YLL emphasises deaths at younger ages. YLD brings in the duration and severity of non-fatal states through disability weights.

QALY versus DALY (draw this table):

| Feature | QALY | DALY |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Health gained (more is better) | Health lost (more is worse) |
| Typical use | Economic evaluation of interventions (CUA) | Burden of disease; some generalised cost-effectiveness (WHO-CHOICE) |
| Weights | Preference scores for health states (often public tariff) | Disability weights (expert/population studies of health loss) |
| Life-expectancy base | Time in states as modelled for the intervention | Standard life table for YLL |
| Age weighting / discounting | Discounting common; age weighting not standard in CUA | Older GBD versions used age weights; current practice should be stated if used |

Do not write "DALY is cost-effectiveness and is best for the health system." CEA uses a single natural unit, which may be life-years, cases, or DALYs averted. CUA uses a generic preference-based unit, usually the QALY. DALYs averted can appear as the denominator of a cost-effectiveness ratio. That does not make DALY a separate, superior method.

Indian context: National Health Policy, 2017 tracks disability-adjusted life years among its impact goals. District answers can use DALY language for burden (which problems are large) and QALY language for choosing between interventions when a CUA exists.

> **EXAM TIP:** Definition → DALY = YLL + YLD with each limb in one line → "one DALY = one healthy year lost" → QALY contrast table (direction, use, weights). Do not invent a national DALY total unless quoting a named report.

[SN]Willingness to pay[/SN]

WILLINGNESS TO PAY
Markets do not price a year of healthy life the way they price a tablet. CBA therefore needs a money value for outcomes. WTP is a stated-preference answer to "what would you pay for this effect?" Related ideas: willingness to accept (compensation to forgo a benefit); discrete choice experiments (choose between hypothetical programmes that vary in attributes, including cost).

WTP may be:
- Compensating variation for a defined gain.
- Elicited by open-ended questions, payment cards, or bidding games.
- Sensitive to ability to pay, starting-point bias, and whether the scenario is believed.

Use in examinations: name WTP as the bridge from health effects to CBA; state that ability to pay can make WTP an unfair social rule if used raw; contrast with extra-welfarist CUA, which measures health and compares it with a threshold based on opportunity cost inside the health budget.

India. Households already reveal something like WTP through OOPE, but OOPE is not a welfare-complete measure: people pay because they have no pooled alternative, not because the price equals value. Do not treat observed OOPE as a WTP tariff for public decisions.

> **EXAM TIP:** Define WTP → role in CBA → two measurement problems (ability to pay, hypothetical bias) → one line contrasting CUA (health unit + threshold) with CBA (money unit). Keep to half a page.

HALE AND RELATED MEASURES
HALE (sometimes taught as having replaced disability-adjusted life expectancy in WHO reporting) summarises population health as years in full health. Sullivan's index (disability-free life expectancy) subtracts years with disability from life expectancy. These are population indicators. They are not interchangeable with QALYs gained by an intervention, which are calculated for a defined alternative versus a comparator.

KEY POINTS
- Natural units serve CEA within one effect.
- QALY = time × preference weight; CUA uses cost per QALY.
- DALY = YLL + YLD; a health-gap unit for burden and some CEA.
- HALE is a population expectation, not an intervention QALY.
- WTP puts a money value on effects for CBA.
- Whose preferences (patient versus public) is a standard viva point.

FORMULAS AND CALCULATIONS
QALY (simple): Σ (years in state i × weight i), discounted if required
QALY gained: area between with-intervention and without-intervention paths
DALY = YLL + YLD
YLL = deaths × standard remaining life expectancy
YLD = cases × duration × disability weight

MNEMONICS
Preference methods: name visual analogue scale (VAS), time trade-off (TTO), and standard gamble (SG).
