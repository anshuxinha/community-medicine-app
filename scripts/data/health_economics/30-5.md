OVERVIEW OF THE CHAPTER
Meaningful comparison examines the additional costs that one programme imposes over another, compared with the additional effects it delivers. That incremental view is the decision rule of cost-effectiveness and cost-utility analysis. This section defines the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER), draws the cost-effectiveness plane, explains dominance, and treats the threshold as an estimate of opportunity cost, not as a moral price of a life.

DEFINITIONS
INCREMENTAL COST-EFFECTIVENESS RATIO (ICER): The extra cost of one alternative over another, divided by the extra effect. ICER = (C1 − C0) / (E1 − E0), where 1 is the new (or more intensive) option and 0 is the comparator. Effects may be life-years, cases, or QALYs. The same algebra with QALYs is the incremental cost-utility ratio; examinations still call it an ICER.

In words: what extra must be paid for each extra unit of health?

COST-EFFECTIVENESS THRESHOLD: The ICER at which the extra health from a programme is judged just worth the extra cost, because it equals the health that would be displaced if those resources were taken from existing care. It is an estimate of opportunity cost inside a constrained budget.

NET MONETARY BENEFIT (NMB): Effect × threshold minus cost. Positive NMB means the programme is cost-effective at that threshold.

NET HEALTH BENEFIT (NHB): Effect minus (cost / threshold). The same rule expressed in health units.

INCREMENTAL, NOT AVERAGE
The average ratio (cost of A / effect of A) compares A with doing nothing at zero cost and zero effect. That is the wrong comparison if current practice already costs money and already produces health. Always subtract the comparator.

Example algebra (numbers are for method, not for citation as Indian policy). Comparator cost 14,200, effect 10.93 QALYs. New strategy cost 15,250, effect 12.66 QALYs. Extra cost 1,050. Extra QALYs 1.73. ICER ≈ 607 per QALY. A further, more expensive strategy is then compared with the new strategy, not with doing nothing.

COST-EFFECTIVENESS PLANE
Plot extra effect on the horizontal axis and extra cost on the vertical axis. The origin is the comparator.

| Quadrant | Extra effect | Extra cost | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| I (north-east) | Higher | Higher | Need a threshold: is extra effect worth extra cost? |
| II (south-east) | Higher | Lower | Dominant: accept (more health, less money) |
| III (south-west) | Lower | Lower | Need a threshold: is the saving worth the health given up? |
| IV (north-west) | Lower | Higher | Dominated: reject |

A dashed threshold line through the origin with slope equal to the threshold (cost per unit effect) splits quadrant I (and III). Points below the line in quadrant I are cost-effective at that threshold. Points above are not.

DOMINANCE
Strong dominance: an option is both more costly and less effective than another. Drop it.
Extended (weak) dominance: an option is more costly per extra effect than a mixture of two others. In a league table of several alternatives, drop extendedly dominated options before computing ICERs along the efficient sequence.

Uncertainty means a result rarely sits in the exact centre of a quadrant. Confidence ellipses and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves (CEACs) describe the probability that an option is cost-effective as the threshold varies. Name them in a long answer; do not invent a curve in a short note.

THRESHOLD AS OPPORTUNITY COST
A threshold is not "what society thinks a life is worth" in the abstract. Under a fixed health budget, spending more on programme A means spending less on programme B. The health lost in B is the opportunity cost. If the extra health from A is smaller than that loss, A should not displace B.

India does not publish a single statutory NICE-style cost-per-QALY threshold for all public purchasing. HTAIn still compares extra cost with extra effect. District decisions use the same logic in natural units: extra rupees per extra child immunised, per extra sputum-positive patient treated, per extra controlled hypertensive, judged against other uses of National Health Mission (NHM) funds.

Do not quote a rupee-per-QALY cut-off as if it were an Indian law.

NMB AND NHB
ICER is a ratio. Ratios misbehave when extra effect is very small or negative. NMB and NHB turn the same information into a linear score: choose the option with the highest NMB (or NHB) at the stated threshold. They also make it easier to add options and to handle uncertainty.

NMB = (E × k) − C
NHB = E − (C / k)
where k is the threshold.

For a short note, formula plus plane is enough.

LEAGUE TABLES AND ABUSE
A league table of cost per QALY from mixed studies is not a menu. Studies differ in perspective, comparator, time horizon, discount rate, and setting. Transferring a ratio from one country to another without checking prices, practice, and epidemiology is a standard flaw (see the ten-question appraisal checklist).

KEY POINTS
- Always incremental: extra cost / extra effect.
- Quadrant II: accept. Quadrant IV: reject. I and III: need k.
- Threshold k estimates opportunity cost of displaced care.
- NMB and NHB are linear forms of the same rule.
- India: HTAIn uses cost-effectiveness; there is no single national k to memorise as law.

FORMULAS AND CALCULATIONS
ICER = (C1 − C0) / (E1 − E0)
NMB = E × k − C
NHB = E − C / k

MNEMONICS
Four quadrants (sentence): North-east asks the price of extra health. South-east is a gift. South-west sells health for money. North-west is a bad bargain.
